# Negotiating Salary: Getting Paid What You're Worth

## Why Negotiation Matters

**Salary negotiation impact over career**:

- Age 25: $60,000 salary (no negotiation)
- Age 25: $66,000 salary (+10% negotiated)
- Compound over 40 years at 3% annual raises
- Age 65: $260,000 vs $288,000
- **Difference: $28,000 lifetime (no compound considered)**

**Actually**: With compounding, difference is $500,000+

Even 5% salary difference = million dollars over career

## Salary Negotiation Statistics

### Real Data
- 50% of people never negotiate
- 70% of those who negotiate get increase
- Average increase: 7-12%
- Time to negotiate: 30 minutes
- ROI: 14x return ($30 minutes × 2000+ ROI)

### Who Negotiates?
- Men: 50% negotiate
- Women: 30% negotiate
- **Why**: Socialization differences

## Preparing for Negotiation

### Step 1: Research Market Rate
**Use tools**:
- Glassdoor: Salary reports
- Payscale: Position-specific
- Levels.fyi: Tech positions
- Bureau of Labor Statistics: Industry data

**Example**:
- Position: Software Engineer
- Location: Austin, TX
- Experience: 5 years
- Market rate: $120,000-140,000

### Step 2: Know Your Value
**Document**:
- Accomplishments (quantified)
- Increase revenue $500,000
- Reduce costs $100,000
- Lead projects (scope)
- Skills unique/valuable

### Step 3: Set Target and Range
- Target: $130,000 (market middle)
- Minimum: $120,000 (won't accept less)
- Stretch: $140,000 (happy if get)
- Share range, not single number (gives flexibility)

### Step 4: Practice Talking Points
- Why you deserve increase
- What value you provide
- Market data supporting ask
- How you help company
- NOT: Need more money for mortgage

## The Negotiation Conversation

### Timing
**Best time to negotiate**:
- After positive performance review (leverage)
- Annual review (standard)
- Job offer (highest leverage)
- After big success (leverage)

**Worst time**:
- During layoffs
- Company struggling
- After mistake
- When emotional

### Opening
Not: "I need a raise"  
Yes: "Based on my contributions and market research, I'd like to discuss my compensation"

**Provide context**:
- Market rate $120-140k (not "I need money")
- Your contributions (revenue, projects, leadership)
- Your ask ($130k)
- Flexibility (willing to discuss)

### Don't Lead with Number
Them: "What do you want?"  
Bad: "$140,000" (too high opener, seems unrealistic)  
Good: "Based on market research and my contributions, I'm thinking $130,000. What does that sound like to you?"

### Listen More Than Speak
- Them: "We budgeted $115,000"
- You: (pause, listen to concerns)
- Identify obstacles
- Address directly

### Common Objections

**"We can't afford it"**:
- Response: "What CAN you afford?"
- Look for middle ground
- OR: Performance bonus instead

**"You're new"**:
- Response: "I've delivered X, Y, Z in 6 months"
- Show value fast
- OR: Revisit in 6 months with timeline

**"Others at your level earn less"**:
- Response: "I've been at top 10% of performance"
- OR: "I bring specialized skills"
- Market rate isn't personal attack

**"Not in budget"**:
- Response: "When will budget be reviewed?"
- Get timeline
- Keep negotiation open

### Closing
"I appreciate your consideration. This reflects my value and market rates. When can we circle back?"

Leaves door open, not final ultimatum (unless job offer)

## Negotiation Strategies

### Anchoring (High)
- Ask high ($140k)
- Counteroffers tend to split difference
- Result: Higher starting point
- Risk: Seem unrealistic

**Best for**: Job offers (high leverage)

### Bracketing (Range)
- Ask for $130k-140k
- Shows flexibility
- Room for discussion
- Less adversarial

**Best for**: Annual reviews

### Performance-based
- "If I deliver X, pay Y"
- Reduces employer risk
- Aligns incentives
- Good for young/new

### Non-monetary
If salary stuck:
- Extra vacation (worth $$)
- Remote work (saves commute)
- Flexible hours
- Professional development budget
- Title change (future leverage)

## What NOT to Do

❌ **Demand/ultimatum**: "Pay me or I leave" (bridges burned)  
❌ **Emotional**: Get angry or cry (undermines position)  
❌ **Compare to others**: "John makes more" (petty)  
❌ **Personal reasons**: "I need money for mortgage" (not their concern)  
❌ **Threats**: "I have another offer" (unless true, then use it)  
❌ **Overstay**: If rejected, accept or move on (don't stew)  

## Job Offer Negotiation (Highest Leverage)

Negotiating new job offer = Best time

### Steps
1. Get offer in writing
2. Research market rate
3. Thank them, ask for time ("I'd like 24-48 hours")
4. Respond with: "I'm very interested. Based on my experience and market research, I was anticipating $X. Can we discuss?"
5. Be prepared to walk away (best negotiating position)

### Example
- Offer: $100,000
- Market: $120,000
- Your request: $115,000
- Likely result: $110,000
- Better than accepting first offer

## When to Walk Away

**Walk if**:
- Won't budge off insulting offer
- Unwilling to have conversation
- Hostile response to reasonable ask
- Better offers elsewhere

**Don't walk just because**:
- They said no once (ask again later)
- They said "we can't afford it" (maybe they can, some)
- Different from offer (could be negotiable)

## Negotiation Don't-Haves

### No competing offer? (Most cases)
- Don't mention one
- Focus on value you bring
- Market data > Threats

### No job offer in hand? (Current role)
- Leverage: Performance + market rate
- Risk: Could damage relationship
- Be professional

### No market data? (Niche role)
- Use best available
- Tell them: "Based on roles I can find..."
- Reasonable/defensible

## Post-Negotiation

### If Successful
- Get in writing
- Thank them
- Deliver on promises
- In 1 year: Negotiate again

### If Unsuccessful
- Accept gracefully
- Set timeline: "Can we revisit in 6-12 months?"
- Continue strong performance
- Build case for next ask

### If Rejected
- Don't burn bridge
- Continue working
- Look for other opportunities
- Negotiation skill applies everywhere

## Negotiation Outcomes

**Scenario A: Ask $130k**:
- Company budgeted $120k
- Likely offer: $125k (split difference)
- Better than $120k!

**Scenario B: Accept first offer**:
- Stuck at $120k
- No effort = No gain

## Key Takeaway
Most people don't negotiate (miss out). Those who do get 7-12% increase (14x ROI on effort). Preparation crucial: know market rate, your value, target range. Lead with market data and accomplishments (not personal need). Listen to objections, address directly. Best leverage: job offers (use it). Walk away if insulting. Over 40-year career, small salary increase = massive lifetime wealth. Negotiation is professional, not personal. Getting paid what you're worth = Smart financial decision.
