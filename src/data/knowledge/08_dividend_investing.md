# Dividend Investing: Income from Your Stocks

## What is a Dividend?

A dividend is a payment companies make to shareholders from profits. Instead of reinvesting ALL profits, companies distribute some as dividends to reward investors.

## How Dividends Work

**Example: Apple Stock**
- You own 100 shares of Apple
- Apple decides to pay $0.24 dividend per share per quarter
- You receive: 100 × $0.24 = $24 quarterly
- Per year: $24 × 4 = $96 annual dividend

## Dividend Metrics

### Dividend Yield
**Annual Dividend ÷ Stock Price = Dividend Yield**

Example:
- Annual Dividend: $1.00
- Stock Price: $50
- Dividend Yield: $1.00 ÷ $50 = **2%**

For $10,000 invested: $10,000 × 2% = **$200/year in dividends**

### Dividend Growth
How much the dividend increases year-over-year:
- Dividend Aristocrats: Increased dividends for 25+ years
- Dividend Kings: Increased dividends for 50+ years
- These are mature, profitable companies

## Types of Dividend Payments

### Cash Dividends (Most Common)
- Payments in cash
- Taxed when received
- Can reinvest automatically (DRIP)

### Stock Dividends
- Receive additional shares instead of cash
- No tax when issued
- Good for compounding

### Special Dividends
- Extra dividend from major profit or sale
- Not guaranteed
- One-time payment

## Dividend Reinvestment (DRIP)

**Dividend Reinvestment Plan**: Automatically reinvest dividends to buy more shares

Example:
- Year 1: Own 100 shares, receive $100 dividend
- DRIP enabled: $100 buys more shares (say 2 shares at $50)
- Year 2: Own 102 shares
- More shares = More dividends = Compounding!

Over 30 years, DRIP can significantly increase returns due to compounding.

## Dividend vs Growth Stocks

### Dividend Stocks
- Mature companies (Microsoft, Coca-Cola, Utilities)
- Lower price appreciation
- Regular income (2-4% yield)
- Lower volatility
- Good for: Income needs, retirees

### Growth Stocks
- Younger companies (Tesla, Amazon, Tech)
- Reinvest profits into business
- No/low dividends
- High price appreciation potential
- High volatility
- Good for: Long-term wealth building, young investors

## Tax Implications

### Qualified Dividends
- Lower tax rate (0%, 15%, or 20% depending on income)
- Need to hold stock 60+ days around dividend date
- Most individual stock dividends qualify

### Non-Qualified Dividends
- Taxed as ordinary income
- Higher tax rate
- Real estate investment trusts (REITs)
- Some bond funds

### Tax-Advantaged Accounts
- 401(k), IRA, Roth IRA: No tax on dividends
- Dividends compound tax-free
- Another reason to use retirement accounts!

## Dividend Yield Red Flags

⚠️ **Unusually High Yield** (6-8%+):
- Company struggling
- Dividend not sustainable
- May get cut

⚠️ **Rising Yield Without Price Increase**:
- Stock price falling
- Dividend may be cut

⚠️ **Dividend Cut Potential**:
- Research company health
- Check payout ratio (dividends ÷ earnings)
- 40-50% is sustainable
- 70%+ is risky (less room for cuts)

## Dividend Aristocrats & Kings

**Best dividend-paying stocks**:
- Dividend Aristocrats: 25+ years of increases
- Examples: Procter & Gamble, Coca-Cola, Johnson & Johnson
- Proof of sustainable business model

## Dividend Investing Strategies

### Income Focus
- Own dividend stocks to live on income
- Good for retirees
- Need significant capital ($1M+) for living expenses

### Growth with Income
- Mix of dividend + growth stocks
- Reinvest dividends for compounding
- Good for building wealth

### Diversified Dividend Portfolio
- Don't put eggs in one basket
- Mix sectors, cap sizes, geographies
- Dividend index funds/ETFs simpler

## Pros of Dividend Investing

✅ **Passive income stream**  
✅ **Proven profitable companies**  
✅ **Compounding through reinvestment**  
✅ **Lower volatility than growth stocks**  
✅ **Behavioral benefit** (harder to panic sell income stocks)  

## Cons of Dividend Investing

❌ **Lower total returns vs growth stocks** (historically)  
❌ **Dividend cuts possible**  
❌ **Tax considerations**  
❌ **Temptation to spend income**  
❌ **Limited price appreciation**  

## Key Takeaway
Dividends are profits companies share with shareholders. Dividend yields (typically 2-4%) provide passive income. Reinvesting dividends through DRIPs creates powerful compounding. Dividend-paying companies tend to be stable, profitable businesses. Good for both income and wealth building, especially in tax-advantaged accounts.
