# Understanding Risk: How Much Can You Handle?

## What is Investment Risk?

Risk is the possibility of losing money or not achieving expected returns. It's fundamental to investing: higher potential returns come with higher risk.

## Types of Investment Risk

### Market Risk (Systematic Risk)
- Affects entire market
- Caused by: Recessions, wars, pandemics, interest rate changes
- **Cannot be diversified away**
- Examples: 2008 financial crisis, 2020 COVID crash

### Company-Specific Risk (Unsystematic Risk)
- Affects individual companies
- Caused by: Poor management, product failures, scandals
- **CAN be diversified away**
- Examples: Tesla scandal, Facebook privacy issues

### Interest Rate Risk
- Bond prices fall when rates rise
- Bond prices rise when rates fall
- Affects fixed-income investments

### Inflation Risk
- Your returns don't keep up with inflation
- $100 today ≠ $100 in 10 years (inflation reduces value)
- Bonds particularly vulnerable to inflation

### Liquidity Risk
- Cannot quickly sell investment
- Some assets harder to sell than others
- Example: Real estate takes months to sell; stocks sell in seconds

### Credit Risk
- Borrower cannot repay (corporate/government bonds)
- Rating agencies help assess this risk

## Risk Tolerance: How Much Risk Can YOU Handle?

### Factors Affecting Risk Tolerance

**Age**: Younger = can take more risk (time to recover)  
**Time Horizon**: Longer = can take more risk  
**Income Stability**: Stable = can take more risk  
**Financial Goals**: Retirement vs. college savings = different risk levels  
**Temperament**: How well do you sleep with volatility?  
**Emergency Fund**: Needed before taking investment risk  

## Risk and Return Relationship

**Conservative Portfolio** (Low Risk):
- 30% stocks, 70% bonds
- Average return: 4-5%/year
- Volatility: Small fluctuations
- Good for: Near-retirees, risk-averse investors

**Moderate Portfolio** (Medium Risk):
- 60% stocks, 40% bonds
- Average return: 7-8%/year
- Volatility: Medium fluctuations
- Good for: Most working-age investors

**Aggressive Portfolio** (High Risk):
- 90% stocks, 10% bonds
- Average return: 9-11%/year
- Volatility: Large fluctuations
- Good for: Young investors, high risk tolerance

## Volatility: The Measure of Risk

**Volatility** = How much prices fluctuate

Example:
- Stock A: $100 → $110 → $95 → $115 (swings widely) = High volatility
- Stock B: $100 → $101 → $102 → $103 (steady) = Low volatility

High volatility = Scarier ride but potentially higher returns  
Low volatility = Smoother ride but potentially lower returns

## Sequence of Returns Risk

**Important for retirees**: The ORDER of returns matters
- If market crashes right after you retire, harder to recover
- If market rises first, crashes later less impactful

## Mitigating Risk

1. **Diversification**: Spread across many investments
2. **Dollar-cost averaging**: Regular investing reduces timing risk
3. **Asset allocation**: Mix of stocks, bonds, other assets
4. **Rebalancing**: Maintain target allocation
5. **Long-term horizon**: Time reduces impact of volatility
6. **Emergency fund**: 3-6 months expenses reduces need to sell investments

## Key Takeaway
All investing involves risk. Match your portfolio risk to your age, goals, time horizon, and temperament. Young investors can handle volatility; near-retirees should not. Diversification and proper asset allocation reduce specific risks.
