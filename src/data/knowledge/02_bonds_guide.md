# Understanding Bonds: Fixed Income Investing

## What is a Bond?
A bond is a loan you give to a company or government. In exchange, they promise to pay you interest and return your principal at maturity. Think of it as an IOU - you lend money, they pay interest.

## Bond Components

### Principal (Face Value)
The amount borrowed. Example: $1,000 bond = you loaned $1,000

### Coupon Rate
The interest rate paid annually. Example: 5% coupon on $1,000 = $50/year interest

### Maturity Date
When the borrower returns your principal. Example: 10-year bond matures in 10 years

### Yield
The effective return you get considering:
- Current price (may be different from face value)
- Coupon payments
- Time to maturity

## Types of Bonds

### Government Bonds
- **US Treasury**: Backed by US government (safest)
- **Municipal**: Issued by cities/states (often tax-free)
- **International**: From foreign governments

### Corporate Bonds
- Issued by companies
- Higher yield than government bonds
- Higher risk than government bonds

### Bond Ratings
**AAA (Safest)** → BBB (Investment Grade) → BB (Speculative/Junk) → CCC (High Risk)

## Why Invest in Bonds?

1. **Predictable Income**: Fixed coupon payments
2. **Lower Risk**: Generally safer than stocks
3. **Stability**: Less volatile than stocks
4. **Diversification**: Move in opposite direction of stocks
5. **Capital Preservation**: Good for conservative investors

## Risks of Bond Investing

1. **Interest Rate Risk**: If interest rates rise, bond prices fall
2. **Credit Risk**: Borrower might default
3. **Inflation Risk**: Inflation erodes your returns
4. **Liquidity Risk**: May be difficult to sell
5. **Low Returns**: Lower returns than stocks historically

## Bond Prices vs Interest Rates

**Inverse Relationship**:
- Interest rates ↑ → Bond prices ↓ (new bonds pay more)
- Interest rates ↓ → Bond prices ↑ (old bonds paying more are valuable)

## Duration
Measures bond price sensitivity to interest rate changes. Higher duration = more sensitive to rate changes.

## Key Takeaway
Bonds provide predictable income and stability but with lower returns and interest rate risk. Good for conservative portfolios and reducing overall risk.
