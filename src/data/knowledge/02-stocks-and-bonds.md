# Stocks and Bonds Explained

## Stocks (equities)

When you buy a stock, you own a small piece of a company. Stock prices go up and down based on the company’s performance, market sentiment, and broader economic conditions. Stocks have historically offered higher long-term returns than bonds but with more short-term volatility.

- **Common stock**: Usually gives voting rights and potential dividends.
- **Dividends**: Some companies pay a portion of profits to shareholders periodically.

## Bonds

A bond is a loan you make to a company or government. In return, the issuer pays you interest and repays the principal at maturity. Bonds are generally less volatile than stocks but typically offer lower long-term returns.

- **Coupon**: The interest rate the bond pays.
- **Maturity**: The date when the principal is repaid.
- **Risk**: Government bonds are often considered lower risk than corporate bonds; higher risk usually means higher yield.

## Why hold both?

A mix of stocks and bonds can balance growth potential and stability. Younger investors often hold more stocks; those nearer retirement may increase bonds to reduce volatility. This is called asset allocation.
