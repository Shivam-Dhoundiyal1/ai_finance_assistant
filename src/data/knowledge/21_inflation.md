# Inflation: How Money Loses Value

## What is Inflation?

Inflation is when prices of goods and services rise over time. Your money buys less than before.

## Example
- 1970: Gallon of gas = $0.36
- 2024: Gallon of gas = $3.50
- Inflation over 54 years

## How Inflation is Measured

### CPI (Consumer Price Index)
Tracks prices of typical goods/services:
- Housing, food, gas, healthcare, clothing

### Current Inflation
2024: ~3% per year (down from peak 8% in 2022)

### Historical Average
2-3% per year is normal

## Why Inflation Happens

### 1. More Money in Economy
Federal Reserve prints money → More dollars chasing same goods → Prices rise

### 2. Supply Shortage
Not enough goods → Prices rise to ration demand

### 3. Cost Increases
Labor, materials costs increase → Companies raise prices

### 4. Expectations
If people expect inflation → Demand rises → Prices rise (self-fulfilling)

## Impact on Savings

### Savings Erosion Example
- Save $10,000 in bank (0% interest)
- Inflation: 3%/year
- Year 1: $10,000 worth → $9,700 worth in today's dollars
- Year 10: $10,000 worth → $7,374 worth in today's dollars

**Result**: Your money loses 26% value in 10 years!

## Impact on Investments

### Good for Stocks
- Companies raise prices with inflation
- Earnings increase with inflation
- Stock prices typically track inflation
- Good long-term inflation hedge

### Bad for Bonds
- Bond pays fixed % (e.g., 4%)
- If inflation 5% → Real return negative!
- Rising inflation → bond prices fall
- Bad inflation hedge

### Good for Real Estate
- Property prices rise with inflation
- Rent increases with inflation
- Mortgage payment fixed (less burden over time)
- Good inflation hedge

## Real vs Nominal Returns

### Nominal Return
What you see:
- Stock up 10%
- Bond paying 4%
- Savings 0.5%

### Real Return
After inflation adjustment:
- Stock up: 10% - 3% inflation = **7% real**
- Bond: 4% - 3% inflation = **1% real**
- Savings: 0.5% - 3% inflation = **-2.5% real** (losing money!)

## Historical Inflation

### Low Inflation (Good for Savers)
- 1995-2020: Average 2% inflation
- Your savings kept value
- Bonds paid decent returns

### High Inflation (Bad for Savers)
- 1970s: 7-8% inflation
- Savers' money evaporated
- Bonds got crushed
- Real estate soared

### Recent (2021-2023)
- 2022: 8% inflation (peak)
- Inflation eroded savings quickly
- Bonds declined sharply
- Stocks declined
- Worst combo

## Inflation Protection Strategies

### 1. Stocks
- Long-term stock returns: 10% (beats 3% inflation)
- Real return: 7%/year
- Best inflation hedge for long-term

### 2. Real Estate
- Prices and rents rise with inflation
- Mortgage payment fixed (decreases burden)
- Excellent inflation hedge

### 3. Inflation-Protected Securities (TIPS)
**Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities**:
- Principal adjusts with inflation
- Interest payment adjusts with inflation
- Always matches inflation
- Low real return (~1-2%)
- Perfect hedge but low returns

### 4. Commodities
- Oil, gold, agricultural products
- Rise in inflationary periods
- Volatile
- Good portfolio diversifier

### 5. Short-Term Bonds
- Rise quickly when rates rise
- Better than long-term bonds in inflation
- Lower yields though

### 6. Avoid Holding Cash
- Cash loses value fastest to inflation
- Only hold 3-6 months expenses
- Invest remainder

## Who Benefits/Loses from Inflation?

### Lose
- Savers (cash loses value)
- Fixed-income earners (salary not keeping up)
- Bond owners (bonds lose value)
- Cash businesses (inventory costs rise)

### Benefit
- Borrowers (debt becomes easier to repay)
- Stock owners (companies raise prices, earnings grow)
- Real estate owners (property appreciates)
- Employees with leverage (can demand raises)

## Controlling Inflation: Fed Policy

### How Central Bank Controls Inflation
**Raise Interest Rates**:
- Borrowing more expensive
- Spending decreases
- Inflation decreases
- But: Recession risk

**Lower Interest Rates**:
- Borrowing cheaper
- Spending increases
- Inflation increases
- But: Can cause excessive inflation

## Inflation Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Low Inflation (2%)
- **Winners**: Stocks, real estate, borrowers
- **Losers**: Savers, bond owners
- **Best allocation**: 70/30 stocks/bonds

### Scenario 2: High Inflation (6%+)
- **Winners**: Real estate, commodities, TIPS
- **Losers**: Bonds, cash, savers
- **Best allocation**: Real estate, commodities, stocks

### Scenario 3: Deflation (Prices Fall)
- **Winners**: Bonds, cash, savers
- **Losers**: Stocks, real estate, borrowers
- **Best allocation**: Bonds, cash

## Managing Inflation in Your Portfolio

### Diversification Approach
- 40% Stocks (inflation hedge)
- 40% Real Estate (inflation hedge)
- 15% Bonds (returns when rates rising)
- 5% Commodities (inflation hedge)

### Simple Approach
- 100% Index Funds (stocks naturally hedge)
- Let time and gains handle inflation

### Conservative Approach
- 60% Stocks
- 40% Bonds/TIPS
- Accept lower real returns

## Key Takeaway
Inflation erodes purchasing power. Stocks are best long-term inflation hedge. Bonds lose value in inflation. Cash is worst in inflation (avoid). Real estate excellent inflation hedge. Historical 10% stock returns beat 3% inflation for real returns. Diversification handles various inflation scenarios. Don't obsess over inflation - long-term stock investing beats it naturally.
