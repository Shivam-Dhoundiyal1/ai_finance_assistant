# Asset Allocation: The Key to Balanced Investing

## What is Asset Allocation?

Asset allocation is dividing your portfolio among different asset classes (stocks, bonds, cash, real estate) based on your goals, risk tolerance, and time horizon.

It's often more important than picking individual stocks!

## Asset Classes

### Stocks (Equities)
- **US Stocks**: Large, mid, small-cap
- **International Developed**: Europe, Japan, Australia
- **Emerging Markets**: China, India, Brazil (higher risk, growth potential)

### Bonds (Fixed Income)
- **Government Bonds**: US Treasury, safest
- **Corporate Bonds**: Company bonds, higher yield
- **Municipal Bonds**: Tax-free for local investors
- **International Bonds**: Foreign country bonds

### Real Assets
- **Real Estate (REITs)**: Real Estate Investment Trusts
- **Commodities**: Gold, oil, agriculture (inflation hedge)

### Cash/Money Market
- **Savings Accounts**: Liquid, safe, low return
- **Money Market Funds**: Very safe, slightly higher return
- **Short-term bonds**: Very safe, modest return

### Alternative Investments
- Cryptocurrency
- Peer-to-peer lending
- Hedge funds

## Sample Asset Allocations by Age

### Age 25-35 (Aggressive)
- 85% Stocks
  - 60% US Stocks
  - 25% International
- 15% Bonds
- 0% Cash/Real Estate

**Rationale**: 40+ years to retirement, can handle volatility

### Age 35-50 (Moderate-Aggressive)
- 70% Stocks
  - 50% US Stocks
  - 20% International
- 25% Bonds
- 5% Cash/Real Estate

**Rationale**: 20-30 years to retirement, balancing growth and stability

### Age 50-60 (Moderate)
- 55% Stocks
  - 40% US Stocks
  - 15% International
- 35% Bonds
- 10% Cash/Real Estate

**Rationale**: 10-20 years to retirement, more stability needed

### Age 60+ (Conservative)
- 40% Stocks
  - 30% US Stocks
  - 10% International
- 50% Bonds
- 10% Cash/Real Estate

**Rationale**: Spending in retirement, need stability and income

## Key Principles

### 1. Time Horizon Matters
- 40+ years to retirement: Can be aggressive (more stocks)
- 10 years to college: Moderate allocation
- 5 years to purchase home: Conservative (more bonds)

### 2. Age-Based Rule of Thumb
**"100 Minus Your Age" Rule**:
- Age 30 → 70% stocks, 30% bonds
- Age 50 → 50% stocks, 50% bonds
- Age 70 → 30% stocks, 70% bonds

**"110/120 Minus Your Age" Rule** (Modern, longer lives):
- Age 30 → 80-90% stocks
- Age 50 → 60-70% stocks
- Age 70 → 40-50% stocks

### 3. Diversification Within Classes
- Stocks: Mix of US, international, sizes
- Bonds: Mix of government, corporate, maturities
- Don't put all in one asset

## Rebalancing

Asset allocation drifts over time as different assets perform differently.

**Example**:
- Target: 60% stocks, 40% bonds
- After good year: 70% stocks, 30% bonds (stocks outperformed)
- Action: Sell some stocks, buy bonds to get back to 60/40

**When to Rebalance**:
- Annually (most common)
- When allocation drifts 5% or more
- After major market moves

**Benefits**:
- Maintains risk level
- "Buy low, sell high" (rebalancing naturally does this)
- Prevents over-concentration

## Asset Allocation Methods

### Age-Based Allocation
- Simple, automatic
- Adjusts as you age
- No thinking required

### Risk-Based Allocation
- Based on risk tolerance
- Conservative, Moderate, Aggressive choices
- Better if risk tolerance doesn't match age

### Goal-Based Allocation
- Different allocations for different goals
- College fund: Moderate
- Retirement: Aggressive (young) → Conservative (approaching)
- Home down payment: Conservative

## Implementation Options

### DIY Approach
- Choose your allocation
- Build portfolio with individual stocks/bonds
- Requires research and rebalancing

### Target-Date Funds (Easiest)
- One fund that automatically adjusts
- Based on target retirement year
- Fund becomes more conservative as you age
- Excellent for 401(k)s and IRAs

### Robo-Advisors (Semi-Automated)
- Algorithm chooses allocation
- Automatically rebalances
- Low fees
- Examples: Betterment, Wealthfront

### Financial Advisor (Full Service)
- Professional builds allocation
- Ongoing management
- Higher fees
- Good for complex situations

## Common Mistakes

❌ **Ignoring bonds when young**: Some bond diversification even at 25  
❌ **Too aggressive near retirement**: Need stability when spending  
❌ **Not rebalancing**: Allocation drifts, increasing risk  
❌ **Changing allocation based on news**: Panic selling is costly  
❌ **Overcomplicating**: Simple allocation beats complex  

## Key Takeaway
Asset allocation is the most important investment decision. Simple, diversified allocations based on age and risk tolerance beat complex strategies. Use target-date funds or simple allocation (100 minus age in stocks) to keep it simple. Rebalance annually. Don't panic and change allocation based on market news.
