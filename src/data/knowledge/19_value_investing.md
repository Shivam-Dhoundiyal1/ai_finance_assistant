# Value Investing: Buying Cheap Assets

## What is Value Investing?

Value investing is buying assets trading below their intrinsic value. You're looking for stocks where market price < true worth.

## Concept: Price vs Value

### Price
What the market currently charges for stock.

### Intrinsic Value
What you think stock is actually worth based on fundamentals.

### Margin of Safety
Discount between price and value.

**Example**:
- Current price: $50
- Your analysis: Worth $100
- Margin of safety: 50%
- Decision: Buy at $50 (gets $100 worth)

## How to Identify Cheap Stocks

### P/E Ratio (Price-to-Earnings)
**P/E = Stock Price ÷ Annual Earnings Per Share**

Example:
- Stock price: $100
- Earnings per share: $10
- P/E ratio: 10

**Interpretation**:
- P/E of 10: Cheap (paying $10 for every $1 of earnings)
- P/E of 25: Expensive (paying $25 for every $1 of earnings)
- Market average: P/E of 15-20

**Value investor threshold**: P/E < 15

### Price-to-Book Ratio
**P/B = Stock Price ÷ Book Value Per Share**

Book value = Assets - Liabilities (what company owns)

Example:
- Stock price: $50
- Book value: $100
- P/B ratio: 0.5 (trading at 50% of book value)

**Interpretation**:
- P/B < 1.0: Cheap (trading below liquidation value!)
- P/B > 1.0: Premium (market values growth)
- Very cheap: P/B < 0.5

### Dividend Yield
**Yield = Annual Dividend ÷ Stock Price**

Example:
- Annual dividend: $5
- Stock price: $50
- Yield: 10%

**Interpretation**:
- Yield > 6%: Possibly cheap (or dividend cut coming)
- Yield 4-6%: Value range
- Yield < 2%: No dividend income

## Value Investing Principles (Warren Buffett)

### 1. Understand the Business
Don't invest in things you don't understand.

**Good**: Coca-Cola (understand soft drinks)  
**Bad**: Biotech (maybe don't understand science)

### 2. Look for Competitive Advantage
Companies with "moat" (hard to compete against):

**Examples**:
- Coca-Cola: Brand loyalty, distribution network
- Apple: Ecosystem lock-in
- Microsoft: Enterprise software lock-in
- Facebook: Network effects

**Question**: Could a new competitor take market share? If not → moat exists

### 3. Buy Understandable Companies
Only invest in straightforward businesses:

**Good**: Retail, insurance, utilities, manufacturing  
**Bad**: Hedge funds, complex derivatives, biotech startups

### 4. Strong Financial Health
- Low debt
- Strong cash flow
- Profitable (or path to profitability)
- Consistent earnings

### 5. Reasonable Price
- Margin of safety 20-30% minimum
- Not buying at peak
- Patience for bargains

## Value Metrics

### Price-to-Free-Cash-Flow
**PFCF = Stock Price ÷ Free Cash Flow Per Share**

Free cash flow = Cash earned - Money invested in growth

**Why it matters**: Companies manipulate earnings, can't fake cash

**Good threshold**: PFCF < 15

### PEG Ratio
**PEG = P/E Ratio ÷ Earnings Growth Rate**

Adjusts P/E for growth:
- Biotech: High growth, high P/E justified
- Utility: Low growth, low P/E expected

**Interpretation**:
- PEG < 1.0: Undervalued (cheap relative to growth)
- PEG > 1.5: Overvalued

### Return on Equity (ROE)
**ROE = Net Income ÷ Shareholder Equity**

How efficiently company uses shareholder money:

- ROE > 15%: Excellent (generating returns)
- ROE 10-15%: Good
- ROE < 10%: Mediocre

## Value Investing Strategy

### Step 1: Screen for Candidates
Find stocks meeting criteria:
- P/E < 15
- P/B < 1.5
- ROE > 15%
- Dividend yield > 3%
- Debt < 50% of equity

### Step 2: Fundamental Analysis
Deep dive into business:
- Understand competitive advantage
- Look at 10-year earnings history
- Check industry trends
- Review management quality
- Read annual reports

### Step 3: Valuation
- Calculate intrinsic value multiple ways
- Compare to current price
- Estimate margin of safety
- Decide if buy-worthy

### Step 4: Purchase
- Buy when price < intrinsic value
- Build position over time
- Don't go all-in on one stock
- Diversify (10-20 stocks minimum)

### Step 5: Hold Long-Term
- Value investors hold 5-10+ years
- Let compounding work
- Resist urge to trade
- Think like owner, not trader

## Value Investing Challenges

### Takes Significant Time
- Research required
- Understand businesses
- Analyze financials
- Not passive

### Requires Discipline
- Must buy when unpopular
- Must hold through downturns
- Must ignore hype
- Boring execution

### Value Trap Risk
Cheap for a reason:
- Company truly broken
- Industry declining
- Competitive disadvantage
- Result: Gets cheaper, not cheaper

### Underperformance Periods
Value lags growth (tech boom):
- 2010-2020: Growth crushed value
- Value investor's returns: 7-8%
- Growth investor's returns: 12-15%
- Patient investors know: Cycles swing both ways

## Value Investing Success Stories

### Buffett's Approach
- Invested in undervalued companies
- Held for decades
- Compounded 19%+ annually for 50+ years
- Became billionaire

### Charlie Munger
- Berkshire partner
- Same value principles
- Consistent outperformance
- Emphasis on understanding business

### Priceline Investment
- Buffett bought at $50 (2011)
- Trading $4,000+ (2024)
- 80x return in 13 years
- Classic value investing

## Pitfalls to Avoid

❌ **Falling in love with stock**: Ignore new bad news  
❌ **Ignoring debt**: High-debt companies dangerous  
❌ **Paying for value**: Buying expensive "cheap" stocks  
❌ **Single stock concentration**: 50% portfolio in one stock  
❌ **Ignoring industry**: Entire industry may decline  

## Key Takeaway
Value investing buys assets at discount to intrinsic value. Requires understanding business, analyzing fundamentals, patience for bargains. Warren Buffett's strategy: Buy cheap, hold long, get rich. Outperformance comes from discipline and long-term holding, not clever trading. For most: Value index funds provide similar benefits without individual stock risk.
