# IRAs: Individual Retirement Accounts

## What is an IRA?

An IRA (Individual Retirement Account) is a personal retirement savings account with tax advantages. Unlike 401(k)s, you open an IRA on your own, not through an employer.

## IRA Types

### Traditional IRA

**Contributions**:
- Pre-tax (tax deductible)
- $7,000/year (age <50)
- $8,000/year (age 50+ - catch-up)
- Reduces your taxable income today

**Growth**:
- Tax-deferred (no taxes on earnings until withdrawal)
- Invest in any stocks, bonds, funds, etc.

**Withdrawals**:
- Taxed as ordinary income
- 10% penalty before age 59.5 (with exceptions)
- Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs) starting age 72-73

**Best for**: People who want tax deduction today and expect lower tax bracket in retirement

### Roth IRA

**Contributions**:
- After-tax (NOT tax deductible)
- $7,000/year (age <50)
- $8,000/year (age 50+ - catch-up)
- No tax deduction today

**Growth**:
- Tax-free (all earnings are tax-free)
- Invest in any stocks, bonds, funds, etc.

**Withdrawals**:
- Tax-free (all withdrawals are tax-free)
- Can withdraw contributions anytime (penalty-free)
- No Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs) - keep growing until death
- Can pass to heirs tax-free

**Best for**: People who expect higher tax bracket in retirement or want tax-free growth

**Income Limits** (2024):
- Single: Phases out starting $146,000
- Married: Phases out starting $230,000
- Cannot contribute if income too high

## Comparing Traditional vs Roth

| Feature | Traditional | Roth |
|---------|-------------|------|
| Tax Deduction | Yes (today) | No |
| Tax-Free Growth | No (tax-deferred) | Yes |
| Tax-Free Withdrawal | No | Yes |
| Age 59.5 Required? | Yes | No (withdraw anytime) |
| RMD Required? | Yes (age 72-73) | No |
| Income Limits? | No | Yes |
| Contributed with | Pre-tax $$ | After-tax $$ |

## SEP IRA (Self-Employed)

For self-employed people or small business owners:
- Contribution limit: Up to 25% of net self-employment income
- Maximum: $69,000/year (2024)
- Simple administration
- Pre-tax contributions

## Solo 401(k) (Self-Employed)

For self-employed with no employees:
- Higher contribution limits than SEP IRA
- Can do both employee and employer contributions
- More complex administration
- More investment control

## IRA Advantages

✅ **Roth offers tax-free growth and withdrawals**  
✅ **Traditional offers tax deduction today**  
✅ **Complete investment control** (any stocks, bonds, funds)  
✅ **High contribution limits** (especially with 401k + IRA combo)  
✅ **Portable** (can move between institutions)  
✅ **No employer needed** (personal account)  

## IRA Disadvantages

❌ **Lower contribution limits than 401(k)**  
❌ **No employer match** (only personal contributions)  
❌ **Roth has income limits**  
❌ **Early withdrawal penalties** (some exceptions)  
❌ **Must have earned income** to contribute  

## Strategic IRA Use

**Best Case: Max Out All Sources**
1. Maximize 401(k) for employer match (free money)
2. Maximize Roth IRA (tax-free growth)
3. Return to 401(k) and max it out (more tax savings)
4. If still have money, use taxable brokerage

**Roth Conversion** (Advanced):
- Convert Traditional to Roth
- Pay taxes now, grow tax-free forever
- Good in low-income years

**Backdoor Roth**:
- Contribute to Traditional IRA
- Immediately convert to Roth
- Gets around income limits
- Complex but valuable for high earners

## Key Takeaway
IRAs are crucial retirement accounts. Roth IRAs are excellent for tax-free growth, Traditional IRAs provide tax deduction today. Maximize contributions before using taxable accounts. IRAs combined with 401(k)s provide powerful retirement savings.
