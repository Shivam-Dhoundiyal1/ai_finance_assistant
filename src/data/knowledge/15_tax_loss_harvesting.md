# Tax-Loss Harvesting: Reduce Taxes Legally

## What is Tax-Loss Harvesting?

Tax-loss harvesting is selling an investment at a loss to reduce your taxable income, then buying a similar investment to stay invested. You get tax benefits while maintaining exposure.

## How It Works

**Example**:
1. Buy 100 shares of Apple (VTI) at $100/share = $10,000
2. Price falls to $80/share = $8,000 value
3. Sell at $80 (lock in $2,000 loss)
4. Buy similar fund (SCHB - different US stock fund) at $80
5. Deduct $2,000 loss on taxes

## Tax Benefit Calculation

**$2,000 loss at 24% tax bracket** = $480 tax savings

Your investment:
- Cost basis: $10,000
- Current value: $8,000
- Tax loss: $2,000
- Tax savings: $480
- Net cost: $8,000 - $480 = $7,520 effective cost

## Step 1: Identify Losses

Throughout the year:
- Review portfolio quarterly
- Identify underwater investments
- List potential losses

Example:
- Bought ETF at $50/share
- Now $45/share
- Loss: $5/share

## Step 2: Harvest the Loss

Sell the losing position:
- Sell at current market price
- Lock in capital loss
- Proceeds ready for reinvestment

**Important**: Sell BEFORE year end (must settle by 12/31)

## Step 3: Reinvest Immediately

Buy similar but not "substantially identical" investment:
- Must hold less than 30 days before or after
- Otherwise IRS disallows loss ("wash sale rule")

Example: Tax-Loss Harvest Pairs
- Sell VTI (Vanguard) → Buy SCHB (Schwab) OR FSKAX (Fidelity)
- Sell QQQ (Nasdaq-100) → Buy QQQX (similar but different fund)
- Sell AGG (Bonds) → Buy BND (similar bonds)

## Types of Losses

### Realized Loss
- You sold at a loss
- Loss is locked in
- Can deduct from taxes

### Unrealized Loss
- Position still owns, value down
- Loss not locked in
- Can't deduct yet
- Can harvest at any time

## Tax-Loss Harvesting Limits

### Capital Loss Deduction
- Can deduct up to $3,000/year of capital losses
- Excess carries forward indefinitely
- Example: $10,000 loss → Deduct $3,000/year → 3.33 year harvest period

### Capital Loss Carryforward
If you have $10,000 loss:
- Year 1: Deduct $3,000 (keep $7,000)
- Year 2: Deduct $3,000 (keep $4,000)
- Year 3: Deduct $3,000 (keep $1,000)
- Year 4: Deduct $1,000 (fully used)

### Using Against Capital Gains
If you also have gains:
- Deduct all losses against gains first
- Excess losses (up to $3,000) against regular income

Example:
- Capital gains: $8,000
- Capital losses: $10,000
- Deduct: $8,000 against gains (zero gain)
- Deduct: $2,000 against regular income (reduce taxable income)
- Carryforward: $0 (all used)

## Wash Sale Rule (Critical!)

### The Rule
Cannot buy "substantially identical" security within 30 days before/after sale.

**Timeline**:
- Day -30 to -1: Can't own
- Day 0: Sell at loss
- Day 1 to Day 30: Can't repurchase
- Day 31+: Can repurchase

### Violation Consequence
- Loss disallowed
- Cost basis increased instead
- Loss doesn't disappear, but delayed

### How to Avoid
- Use different fund (similar but not identical)
- Examples:
  - Sell VTI (Vanguard) → Buy SCHB (Schwab)
  - Sell VOO (S&P 500) → Buy SPLG (S&P 500)
  - Sell AGG (Bonds) → Buy BND (Bonds)

### IRS Test for "Substantially Identical"
- Same underlying holdings (approximately)
- Holdings in same proportion
- Different fund = usually acceptable

## Advanced Tax-Loss Harvesting

### Selective Harvesting
Only harvest when:
- Large losses exist
- Alternative fund available
- Won't trigger wash sale
- Tax benefit meaningful

### Tax Bracket Year
Harvest more in high-income year:
- Planning: Higher income year → harvest more losses
- Higher deduction benefit
- Example: Freelancer with high-income year → harvest $5,000 loss = $1,200 saved (24% bracket)

### Spousal Coordination
If married:
- Each spouse has $3,000 limit
- Coordinate harvesting
- $6,000 total deductible

## Common Mistakes

❌ **Missing wash sale window**: Buying same fund immediately  
❌ **Not tracking**: Forgetting which funds are "substantially identical"  
❌ **Leaving money on table**: Not harvesting available losses  
❌ **Over-harvesting**: Assuming loss carries over (it does, but limited)  
❌ **Forgetting reinvestment**: Sitting in cash after harvest
