# Behavioral Finance: Psychology of Investing

## What is Behavioral Finance?

Behavioral finance studies how emotions and cognitive biases affect investment decisions. Most people make worse investment decisions than theory predicts.

## Common Cognitive Biases

### Overconfidence Bias
**Belief**: "I'm better at picking stocks than average"

**Reality**: 85% of professional fund managers underperform index funds over 15+ years. You probably will too.

**Result**: You pick individual stocks, underperform, blame bad luck

**Solution**: Accept you're average. Use index funds.

### Recency Bias
**Belief**: "Recent performance predicts future"

**Reality**: Hot funds cool off. Last year's worst fund is next year's best.

**Example**:
- 2023: Tech stocks up 42% (hottest sector)
- You buy heavily
- 2024: Tech stocks down 5%
- You lose money
- Tech actually recovered in 2025-2026 (but you sold)

**Solution**: Don't chase performance. Stick to allocation.

### Anchoring Bias
**Belief**: "I'll sell when it gets back to what I paid"

**Reality**: Original price is irrelevant

**Example**:
- Buy stock at $100
- Falls to $70
- Could buy more (cheap), or should sell if thesis broken
- Instead: Hold waiting for $100 (arbitrary)
- Lost 5 years, stock is now $60

**Solution**: Forget what you paid. Make decisions based on current value.

### Availability Heuristic
**Belief**: "What I hear about must be important"

**Reality**: News is selected for excitement, not importance

**Example**:
- See news about stock crash
- Panic sell
- Actually should be buying (prices low)
- Miss recovery

**Solution**: Ignore news. Stick to plan.

### Herding Bias
**Belief**: "Everyone is buying, must be right"

**Reality**: Crowds are usually wrong at extremes

**Example**:
- 2021: Everyone buying Tesla, crypto
- You join in
- 2022: Crash (predictable)
- You sold at lows
- Lost money

**Solution**: Do opposite of crowds.

### Sunk Cost Fallacy
**Belief**: "I've already lost $10,000, can't sell now"

**Reality**: Past losses are irrelevant

**Example**:
- Invested $100,000 in bad stock
- Down to $40,000
- Keep holding because "can't accept loss"
- Falls to $10,000
- Could have accepted $40,000 loss when investment thesis broke
- Now lost $90,000

**Solution**: Evaluate investment on its merits, not past losses.

### Optimism Bias
**Belief**: "Bad things happen to others, not me"

**Reality**: You're average. Bad things happen to you too.

**Example**:
- No emergency fund (won't happen to me)
- No insurance (will be fine)
- All stocks no bonds (stocks always go up)
- Then disaster strikes

**Solution**: Plan for bad outcomes. Diversify.

### Loss Aversion
**Belief**: "Pain of losing $100 > pleasure of gaining $100"

**Reality**: True for humans (studied extensively)

**Result**: You hold losers (avoid realizing loss) and sell winners (capture gains)

**Example**:
- Stock down 20%: Hold (hoping recovery)
- Stock up 20%: Sell (locking in small gain)
- Result: More losers, fewer winners
- Underperformance

**Solution**: Pre-decide sell rules. Execute automatically.

## Behavioral Mistakes in Markets

### Market Timing
**Mistake**: Trying to buy low, sell high

**Reality**: 
- Professional algorithms fail at timing
- You definitely will fail
- Missing 10 best days cuts returns 50%+

**Example**:
- 1995-2024: S&P 500 = 10.2% annual
- Miss best 10 days: 6.2% annual
- Miss best 20 days: 4.3% annual
- Miss best 30 days: 2.4% annual

**Solution**: Stay invested. Dollar-cost average.

### Panic Selling
**Mistake**: Selling in downturns

**Historical Data**:
- 1987: Down 22% in one day. Recovered in months.
- 2008: Down 55%. Recovered in 4 years.
- 2020: Down 34% in 23 days. Recovered in 1 year.

**Panic sellers**: Locked in losses, missed recoveries

**Solution**: Have cash. Long time horizon. Ignore news.

### Chasing Performance
**Mistake**: Buying hottest funds/stocks

**Reality**: Outperformers revert to mean

**10-year study**:
- Top 20% funds one decade
- Bottom 20% funds next decade
- Correlation near zero

**Solution**: Buy boring index funds. Rebalance.

### Analysis Paralysis
**Mistake**: Researching forever, never investing

**Reality**: Starting early beats perfect timing

**Example**:
- 2008: Perfect time to invest (market down)
- People researching, waiting for "bottom"
- By 2010: Already recovered
- Missed entire recovery

**Solution**: Get started. Imperfect action beats perfect inaction.

## Behavioral Solutions

### 1. Automate
- Automatic paycheck deductions to 401(k)
- Dollar-cost averaging
- No emotions involved
- Proven effective

### 2. Simplify
- One target-date fund (vs analyzing 100 options)
- One asset allocation (vs tweaking constantly)
- Less to think about = fewer mistakes

### 3. Set Rules
- "I will rebalance annually" (stick to it)
- "I will not check portfolio daily" (removes temptation)
- "I will not sell on bad news" (pre-decide)

### 4. Time Horizon
- Remind yourself: 30-year horizon
- Daily moves don't matter
- Prevents panic selling

### 5. Group Decisions
- Tell friends your plan
- Social commitment helps
- Less likely to abandon

### 6. Financial Advisor
- Behavioral coach effect
- Keeps you from dumb moves
- Often pays for itself (preventing one big mistake)

### 7. Ignore News
- Turn off CNBC
- Stop reading market commentary
- Reduces emotional decisions
- Historically improves returns

## Behavioral Advantages (Good Biases)

### Overconfidence (Positive)
**Belief**: "I can build wealth"

**Why it helps**: Encourages starting
**How to use**: Get started, stay invested 30 years

### Home Bias (Acceptable)
**Belief**: "US stocks safer, I understand them"

**Why it helps**: More comfortable in US allocation
**How to use**: 70% US, 30% international (compromise)

### Familiarity Bias (Use Carefully)
**Belief**: "I like this company, I'll own it"

**Why it helps**: Emotional connection aids long-term holding
**How to use**: Own some individual stocks (10% portfolio) for engagement

## Key Behavioral Finance Lessons

1. **Emotions lose money**: Fear and greed are expensive
2. **Simple beats complex**: Index funds beat stock picking
3. **Automation beats willpower**: Pre-decision commitments work
4. **Boring wins**: Most exciting strategies underperform
5. **Time beats timing**: Stay invested 30 years
6. **Diversification prevents panic**: Safe portfolio = you don't panic sell
7. **Avoid news**: Market commentary is designed to trigger emotions
8. **Find accountability**: Tell someone your plan

## Key Takeaway
Behavioral finance explains why smart people make bad investment decisions. Emotions, overconfidence, and biases cause underperformance. Solutions: automate investing, simplify, ignore news, stay disciplined. The investor who does nothing (index fund, automatic contributions) beats the smart investor who constantly tinkers. Boring discipline beats exciting mistakes.
