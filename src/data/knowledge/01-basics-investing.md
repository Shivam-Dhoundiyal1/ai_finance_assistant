# Basics of Investing

## What is investing?

Investing means putting your money into assets that you expect to grow in value over time. Common assets include stocks, bonds, and funds. Unlike saving (e.g., in a savings account), investing carries more risk but has historically offered higher potential returns over the long term.

## Why invest?

- **Beat inflation**: Over time, prices rise. Cash in a low-interest account may lose purchasing power. Investments that grow faster than inflation help preserve and grow wealth.
- **Build wealth**: Compound growth means your returns can earn returns. Starting early gives your money more time to compound.
- **Reach goals**: Retirement, a home, or education often require more than savings alone. Investing can help you work toward these goals.

## Key principles

1. **Diversification**: Don’t put all your money in one company or one type of asset. Spreading across many investments can reduce risk.
2. **Time horizon**: How long you can leave money invested matters. Longer horizons usually allow more risk (e.g., more stocks) because you have time to recover from downturns.
3. **Risk and return**: Higher potential return usually comes with higher risk. Understanding your risk tolerance helps you choose a mix of assets that you can stick with.
