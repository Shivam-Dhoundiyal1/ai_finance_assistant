# Robo-Advisors: Automated Investing Made Easy

## What is a Robo-Advisor?

A robo-advisor is an automated investment platform:
- You answer questions about goals/risk
- Algorithm builds portfolio
- Automatically manages and rebalances
- Low human interaction
- Low fees

## How They Work

### Step 1: Questionnaire
Answer questions:
- What's your age?
- When do you need the money?
- What's your risk tolerance?
- What are your goals?

### Step 2: Algorithm Creates Portfolio
Based on answers, creates allocation:
- Age 30, aggressive: 90% stocks, 10% bonds
- Age 50, moderate: 60% stocks, 40% bonds
- Age 70, conservative: 40% stocks, 60% bonds

### Step 3: Auto-Rebalancing
Quarterly/annually, portfolio drifts:
- Robo-advisor rebalances automatically
- No user action needed
- Keeps allocation on track

### Step 4: Tax Harvesting
Some platforms auto-harvest tax losses:
- Sell at losses
- Buy similar fund
- Capture tax deductions
- Increase after-tax returns

## Robo-Advisor Features

### Automatic Rebalancing
Portfolio drifts:
- Robo fixes automatically
- You don't have to
- Happens quarterly/annually

### Tax-Loss Harvesting
Sell losses, buy similar fund:
- Betterment/Wealthfront: Daily harvesting
- Adds 0.5-1% annual returns
- May offset 0.25% fee

### Goal Planning
Track multiple goals:
- College fund (5 years)
- House down payment (3 years)
- Retirement (35 years)
- Different allocations for each

### Financial Planning
Estimate retirement needs:
- How much to save monthly
- Retirement readiness
- Path to goals

### Advisor Access
Some platforms offer:
- Chat support (free)
- Video calls (premium)
- Financial advice (limited)

## When Robo-Advisors Make Sense

### Perfect for:
✅ Beginners (simplest start)  
✅ Hands-off investors (set and forget)  
✅ Those under $100k (lower minimum than advisors)  
✅ Young people with long horizons  
✅ People who would otherwise do nothing  

### Not ideal for:
❌ Disciplined DIYers (0.21% fee drag unnecessary)  
❌ Large portfolios ($500k+) use real advisors  
❌ Those seeking complex strategies  
❌ Penny-wise investors (fees matter)  

## Robo-Advisor Risks

### Overlaps with Other Accounts
- 401(k) already has stocks
- Robo might create duplication
- Consider overall allocation

### Simplification Risk
- "Set and forget" works
- Unless life changes
- Need to review goals

### Tax Implications
- Robo harvests losses
- Might create wash sales if not careful
- Generally good but monitor

## Hybrid Approach

Use robo-advisor for specific goals:
- Retirement: Own 401(k)
- House down payment: Robo-advisor (5-year horizon)
- College fund: Robo-advisor (15-year horizon)
- Long-term wealth: Index funds DIY

- Final value: $786,000

## Key Takeaway
Robo-advisors are excellent for beginners and hands-off investors. Automatic rebalancing and tax harvesting add value. Fee of 0.25% is reasonable for most. For wealthy/disciplined: DIY index funds cheaper. For lazy/beginner: Robo-advisor worth it. Better to be in robo with 0.25% fee than in cash earning 0%. Robo-advisors democratized professional management. Consider Schwab/Fidelity's free offerings first.
