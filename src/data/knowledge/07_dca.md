# Dollar-Cost Averaging: Smart Regular Investing

## What is Dollar-Cost Averaging (DCA)?

Dollar-Cost Averaging is investing a fixed amount of money at regular intervals regardless of price. You invest the same dollar amount whether prices are high or low.

## How DCA Works

**Example**: You decide to invest $500 every month in an S&P 500 index fund

| Month | Price/Share | Amount Invested | Shares Purchased |
|-------|-------------|-----------------|------------------|
| January | $100 | $500 | 5 shares |
| February | $80 | $500 | 6.25 shares |
| March | $90 | $500 | 5.56 shares |
| April | $70 | $500 | 7.14 shares |
| May | $85 | $500 | 5.88 shares |
| **Total** | **Average: $85** | **$2,500** | **29.83 shares** |

**Average Cost Per Share**: $2,500 ÷ 29.83 = **$83.84**

## Why DCA Works

### 1. Reduces Market Timing Risk
You don't need to guess the bottom:
- Invest when price is high: Buy fewer shares
- Invest when price is low: Buy more shares
- Average out to reasonable price

### 2. Removes Emotion
Automatic investing prevents:
- Panic selling in downturns
- FOMO buying at peaks
- Hesitation to invest

### 3. Takes Advantage of Volatility
Paradoxically, lower prices are GOOD:
- You're buying more shares when cheap
- Sets you up for bigger gains when price recovers

## DCA vs Lump Sum Investing

### Lump Sum
**If you have $10,000 right now**:

Pros:
- ✅ Money invested immediately
- ✅ Historically performs better (market goes up more than down)
- ✅ Gets you in the market sooner

Cons:
- ❌ Risk of buying at a peak
- ❌ Emotionally harder to handle immediate losses

### Dollar-Cost Averaging
**Invest $833/month over 12 months**:

Pros:
- ✅ Psychologically easier
- ✅ Reduces timing risk
- ✅ Forces discipline

Cons:
- ❌ Miss some early gains (if market is up)
- ❌ Takes longer to be fully invested
- ❌ Small additional fees (more transactions)

## When to Use DCA

**Ideal for**:
- Regular salary/income → automate investing
- Large inheritance → don't want to time it
- Nervous about markets → eases into investing
- Building wealth over time (401k, IRA contributions)

**Consider lump sum if**:
- You have time and won't need money for 10+ years
- Market is historically cheap
- You're emotionally comfortable with volatility

## DCA in Practice

### 401(k) Contributions
- Automatically deducted from paycheck
- Perfect dollar-cost averaging
- You contribute same amount each paycheck regardless of market
- This is DCA in action!

### Monthly Investment Plan
```
Monthly Income: $3,000
Invest: $500/month in index funds
Automatically: Same day each month
Result: DCA over many years
```

### Target-Date Funds
- Automatically invest in diversified portfolio
- Rebalance regularly
- Built-in DCA through regular contributions

## Mathematical Advantage of DCA

When prices are volatile, DCA beats lump sum:
- Lump sum: $10,000 → 100 shares at $100
- DCA: $833/month × 12 months averages to 119 shares at $84 average

**Lower average cost = More shares = Better returns**

## Behavioral Benefits

DCA prevents costly mistakes:
- ❌ Panic selling after crash
- ❌ FOMO buying at peak
- ❌ Trying to time the market
- ✅ Automatic discipline
- ✅ Reduces emotional decisions

## Key Takeaway
Dollar-cost averaging is investing fixed amounts regularly (weekly, monthly, etc.). It reduces timing risk, prevents emotional mistakes, and takes advantage of volatility. Perfect for building wealth through regular income. Combined with diversification and long-term investing, DCA is a powerful strategy for most investors.
