# Risk Tolerance and Goal Planning

## What is risk tolerance?

Risk tolerance is how much volatility or potential loss you can accept in exchange for higher potential returns. It depends on your goals, time horizon, and comfort level.

## Factors that affect it

- **Time horizon**: Longer time until you need the money usually allows more risk (e.g., more stocks).
- **Financial situation**: Stable income and an emergency fund can support taking more investment risk.
- **Personality**: Some people are comfortable with big swings; others prefer steadier, lower-return options.

## Setting financial goals

- **Define goals**: e.g., retirement at 65, down payment in 10 years, emergency fund in 2 years.
- **Estimate cost and timeline**: How much you need and when.
- **Choose a strategy**: Save and invest in a mix of assets that matches your timeline and risk tolerance.
- **Review periodically**: Life changes; adjust your plan and allocation as needed.

## Risk and return

Higher potential return usually comes with higher risk. A balanced approach is to take enough risk to pursue your goals without taking so much that you panic and sell during a downturn. Diversification and a long-term perspective can help.
