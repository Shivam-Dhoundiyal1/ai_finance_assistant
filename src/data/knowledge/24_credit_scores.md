# Credit Scores: Understanding Your Financial Rating

## What is a Credit Score?

A credit score is a number (300-850) representing your creditworthiness. Lenders use it to decide if you'll repay loans and at what interest rate.

## Why Credit Score Matters

### Higher Score = Better Rates
**Borrowing $300,000 mortgage**:
- Score 750: 4% rate = $1,432/month
- Score 650: 5% rate = $1,610/month
- Difference: $178/month = $64,000 over 30 years!

## FICO Score Components (85% of scores used)

### 1. Payment History (35%)
Do you pay on time?

**Positive**:
- ✅ On-time payments (boost score)
- ✅ All bills paid
- ✅ No late payments

**Negative**:
- ❌ Late payments (major hit)
- ❌ Collections (severe hit)
- ❌ Bankruptcy (very severe)

### 2. Credit Utilization (30%)
How much credit are you using?

**Example**:
- Credit limit: $10,000
- Balance: $2,000
- Utilization: 20% (GOOD)

- Credit limit: $10,000
- Balance: $8,000
- Utilization: 80% (BAD)

**Optimal**: Under 10% utilization
**Acceptable**: Under 30%
**Bad**: Over 50%

### 3. Length of Credit History (15%)
How long have you been borrowing?

**Helps score**:
- Long credit history (10+ years)
- Old accounts (keeps average age up)
- Consistent borrowing

**Hurts score**:
- New to credit (under 1 year)
- Closing old accounts (shortens history)
- Very recent credit activity

### 4. Credit Mix (10%)
Variety of credit types

**Good mix**:
- Credit card (revolving)
- Mortgage (installment)
- Auto loan (installment)
- Student loans

**Why it helps**: Shows you can manage different types

### 5. New Credit Inquiries (10%)
Recent credit applications

**Negative**:
- ❌ Multiple inquiries in short time (looks desperate)
- ❌ New accounts (shortens average age)

**Rule**: Hard inquiries drop score 5-10 points temporarily

## Credit Score Ranges

| Score | Rating | Qualification |
|-------|--------|----------------|
| 800+ | Excellent | Best rates, approval easy |
| 750-799 | Very Good | Great rates, approval easy |
| 700-749 | Good | Good rates, approval likely |
| 650-699 | Fair | Moderate rates, approval possible |
| 600-649 | Poor | High rates, approval difficult |
| <600 | Very Poor | Very high rates or denial |

## Improving Credit Score

### Immediate Steps (Days-Weeks)
1. **Pay down balances**: Lower utilization immediately helps
2. **Check for errors**: Dispute wrong items on credit report
3. **Pay all bills on time**: Set up auto-pay

### Short-term (Months)
4. **Stop applying for credit**: Reduce hard inquiries
5. **Keep old accounts open**: Length of history matters
6. **Request credit limit increase**: Reduces utilization ratio

### Long-term (Years)
7. **Build payment history**: 24+ months of perfect payments
8. **Diversify credit**: Get mix of types (if needed)
9. **Let negative items age**: Collections age off after 7 years

## Free Credit Reports

### AnnualCreditReport.com
- One free report per year from each bureau
- Equifax, Experian, TransUnion
- No credit card required
- Only official source

**Strategy**: Get report from one bureau every 4 months
- January: Equifax
- May: Experian
- September: TransUnion
- Monitor throughout year

### Credit Monitoring Services
- Credit Karma: Free credit scores (not FICO)
- Experian: Free FICO score
- Discover: Free FICO score (Discover cardholders)
- Most banks: Free score with account

## Credit Score Myths

### Myth 1: "Checking my score hurts it"
FALSE. Checking your own score = soft inquiry, doesn't hurt.

### Myth 2: "I should close old credit cards"
FALSE. Closing old accounts hurts length of history.

### Myth 3: "Paying off credit card helps score"
PARTIALLY TRUE. Helps utilization, but payment history still matters.

### Myth 4: "My income affects my score"
FALSE. Credit score only reflects payment behavior, not income.

### Myth 5: "I need to carry a balance"
FALSE. Myth causes people to pay interest unnecessarily. Utilization matters, not balance.

## Building Credit From Zero

**If you have no credit history**:

1. **Secured credit card**: Deposit $500, get $500 card
   - Build history safely
   - Graduate to unsecured card after 12+ months

2. **Become authorized user**: On parent/friend's card
   - Get credit history from their account
   - Easier than new card

3. **Credit builder loan**: Small loan ($500-$1,000)
   - Intentionally build history
   - Money deposited in savings (your collateral)
   - Pay back slowly
   - Build payment history

4. **Installment credit**: Auto loan or furniture
   - Adds credit mix
   - Builds history
   - More expensive than credit cards though

## Credit Score for Major Purchases

### Mortgage (Largest Purchase)
- Score 620+: Can qualify
- Score 700+: Good rates
- Score 750+: Best rates
- Down payment required

### Auto Loan
- Score 620+: Can qualify
- Score 700+: Fair rates
- Score 750+: Best rates

### Credit Card
- Score 600+: Can get card
- Score 700+: Better cards
- Score 750+: Premium cards with rewards

## Credit Score and Finances

### Employment
- Some employers check scores (not common)
- Finance/banking/security industry may care
- Doesn't affect most jobs

### Housing
- Landlords often check scores
- Score under 600: Harder to rent
- May require higher deposit

### Insurance
- Insurance companies use credit-based insurance score (different)
- Affects rates
- Worth checking

## Protecting Your Credit

### Monitor for Fraud
- Check credit report regularly
- Alert if accounts you don't recognize
- Dispute quickly

### Freeze Credit (If At Risk)
- Lock credit file
- No one can open new accounts
- Use if identity stolen or afraid of theft

### Identity Theft Protection
- Monitor for new accounts
- Freezing helps prevent
- Insurance available ($10-20/month)

## Key Takeaway
Credit score (300-850) determines borrowing rates. Payment history (35%) and credit utilization (30%) are biggest factors. Keep balances under 10% of credit limits. Pay all bills on time. Check free credit reports yearly. Score improves over time with good behavior (24+ months of perfect payments = major improvement). Building score takes years; protecting it takes vigilance. Monitor regularly, dispute errors, avoid hard inquiries.
