# Financial Goals: Setting and Achieving Targets

## Types of Financial Goals

### Short-term (1 year)
- Emergency fund: $1,000
- Save for vacation: $3,000
- Pay off credit card: $5,000
- Build to $10,000

### Medium-term (2-5 years)
- House down payment: $50,000
- Car purchase: $25,000
- Wedding: $15,000
- Debt payoff: $50,000

### Long-term (5-30+ years)
- Retirement: $1,000,000
- College fund: $200,000
- House payoff: 30 years
- Financial independence: $500,000+

## SMART Goals Framework

### Specific
Not: "Save more money"  
Yes: "Save $500/month for down payment"

Not: "Invest more"  
Yes: "Contribute $10,000/year to 401(k)"

### Measurable
Track progress:
- Current: $5,000 saved
- Target: $50,000 (down payment)
- Progress: 10% complete
- Months remaining: 90

### Achievable
Realistic based on income:
- Income: $5,000/month
- Expenses: $3,500/month
- Available: $1,500/month
- Goal: Save $500/month (achievable)

### Relevant
Aligned with your values:
- Why save $50,000?
- "To buy house, build wealth, stability"
- Makes sense

### Time-Bound
Has deadline:
- Not: "Save $50,000"
- Yes: "Save $50,000 by 2027 (4 years)"

## Goal Setting Exercise

### Step 1: List Goals
Write all financial goals:
- Short-term (1 year)
- Medium-term (2-5 years)
- Long-term (5-30 years)

### Step 2: Prioritize
Rank by importance:
1. Emergency fund (safety)
2. High-interest debt (math)
3. House down payment (lifestyle)
4. Retirement (future)

### Step 3: Calculate Savings Needed
**Time-based calculation**:
- Goal: $50,000 in 4 years
- Monthly needed: $50,000 ÷ 48 = $1,041/month
- Achievable with $1,500/month available? Yes

**Compound interest boost** (if invested):
- Save $500/month
- 6% growth annually
- 4 years: $2,600 interest bonus
- Total: $26,600 vs $24,000 (saved)
- Interest adds $2,600!

### Step 4: Automate
Set up automatic savings:
- Every paycheck: $500 to savings
- No willpower needed
- Automatic discipline

### Step 5: Track Progress
Monitor quarterly:
- Current saved: $15,000 (6 months in)
- Target: $50,000
- Completion: 30%
- Timeline: On track for 2027

## Common Goals and Strategies

### Emergency Fund
**Goal**: 3-6 months expenses

**If expenses**: $3,000/month
**Target**: $9,000-18,000
**Timeline**: 12-24 months

**Strategy**:
- High-yield savings: 4-5% return
- Accessible (not invested)
- Builds before aggressive investing

### House Down Payment
**Goal**: 20% down

**If house**: $300,000
**Target**: $60,000
**Timeline**: 4-5 years

**Strategy**:
- Save aggressively: $1,000-1,500/month
- Keep in low-risk (bonds/savings)
- Timeline too short for stocks (risky)

### Retirement
**Goal**: $1,000,000 by age 65

**If age 35, want $1M at 65**:
**Timeline**: 30 years
**Monthly needed**: $600/month
**Invested at 10% growth**: Compounds to $1,000,000

**Strategy**:
- 401(k) first ($300/month)
- Roth IRA ($300/month)
- Long timeline allows stocks (100%)

### College Fund
**Goal**: $200,000 for college

**If age 8, college age 18**:
**Timeline**: 10 years
**Monthly needed**: $1,200/month
**Or 529 plan**: $1,000/month (tax-free growth)

**Strategy**:
- 529 plan (tax-advantaged)
- 75% stocks / 25% bonds (10 years is long)
- Age-based (automatically de-risks as age approaches)

### Debt Payoff
**Goal**: Pay off $20,000 credit card

**At 18% interest**:
**If minimum $400/month**: 7 years, $8,000 interest
**If $800/month**: 2 years, $2,000 interest
**If $1,500/month**: 1.5 years, $1,000 interest

**Strategy**:
- Pay as much as possible
- Every dollar saved in interest
- Highest priority (worst interest rate)

## Multiple Goal Prioritization

With limited income, how to fund multiple goals?

### Waterfall Approach
1. **Emergency fund**: First $1,000
2. **Match 401(k)**: Must get free money
3. **High-interest debt**: Pay if >8% rate
4. **Continue emergency fund**: 3-6 months
5. **Roth IRA**: Up to $7,000/year
6. **House down payment**: Or other medium goal
7. **Retirement catch-up**: Additional 401(k)
8. **Investing**: After main goals

### Time-based Approach
- Age 25-35: Retirement focus (time value)
- Age 35-50: Mixed (retirement + house)
- Age 50+: Retirement catch-up

### Income-based Approach
**If income** $50,000/year:
- 60% to expenses: $2,500/month
- Emergency fund: 10% ($417/month)
- One goal: 5% ($208/month)

**If income** $100,000/year:
- 60% to expenses: $5,000/month
- Emergency fund: 5% ($417/month)
- Multiple goals: 10% ($833/month)

## Goal Adjustment

### If Behind
- Increase savings (cut expenses)
- Extend timeline (delay goal)
- Accept lower goal (smaller amount)
- Combination of above

### If Ahead
- Increase next goal's pace
- Start new goal
- Celebrate progress!

## Life Stage Goals

### 20s Goals
- Emergency fund
- Start retirement ($100/month)
- Entry-level career
- Mobility prioritized over assets

### 30s Goals
- Emergency fund (complete)
- Retirement catch-up
- House down payment (if wanted)
- College savings start

### 40s Goals
- Retirement on track
- House paid down
- Kids' college funded (partially)
- Solidify wealth

### 50s Goals
- Retirement security verified
- Catch-up contributions
- Major assets protected
- Estate planning

## Key Takeaway
Clear financial goals crucial for discipline and motivation. Use SMART framework (specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound). Prioritize: Emergency fund → High-interest debt → Retirement → Secondary goals. Automate savings (removes emotion). Track quarterly (maintain momentum). Goals motivate behavior: "Save $50k for house" more motivating than "save more." Without goals, money drifts to consumption. With goals, spending becomes intentional. Review and adjust annually.
