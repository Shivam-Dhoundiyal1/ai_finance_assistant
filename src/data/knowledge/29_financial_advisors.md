# Financial Advisors: When to Hire Help

## Types of Financial Advisors

### Fee-Only Advisor (Best Alignment)
Charges flat fee or hourly, nothing else.

- **Fee**: $1,000-$5,000 flat or $150-$400/hour
- **Commission**: None (no conflicts)
- **Best for**: Complex financial plans

**Advantages**:
- ✅ No conflicts of interest
- ✅ Objective advice
- ✅ Can shop around (hourly)

**Disadvantages**:
- ❌ Upfront cost
- ❌ Not ongoing management (usually)

### Fee-Based Advisor
Charges fees + commissions

- **Fee**: 0.5-1.5% annually + commissions
- **Commission**: Selling products (conflict!)
- **Watch for**: Recommending expensive products

**Problem**: Incentive to sell high-commission products (not best for you)

### Commission-Only Advisor
Only makes money selling you products

- **Fee**: $0
- **Commission**: 3-6% on products sold
- **Problem**: HUGE conflict of interest

**Example**: Recommends $100,000 in products, earns $3,000-6,000 commission

**Avoid**: Obvious conflicts

### AUM Advisor (Assets Under Management)
Charges percentage of assets they manage

- **Fee**: 0.5-1.5% annually
- **Model**: Assets to manage = fee income
- **Alignment**: More assets = more fee
- **Best for**: Ongoing management

**Advantage**: Wants portfolio to grow (aligned)  
**Disadvantage**: Might push too much money management

## Fiduciary vs Non-Fiduciary

### Fiduciary (Best for You)
Legally required to act in your best interest.

**Fiduciaries include**:
- Fee-only advisors
- Registered Investment Advisors (RIA)
- Most 401(k) advisors

**Protection**: Can sue if they breach duty

### Non-Fiduciary (Suitability Standard)
Only required to recommend "suitable" products (not necessarily best).

**Non-fiduciaries include**:
- Brokers selling stocks/bonds
- Insurance agents
- Some investment salespeople

**Problem**: Can recommend expensive product over cheaper alternative

**Red flag**: Ask "Are you a fiduciary?" - If hesitation = probably not

## When You Need a Financial Advisor

### Good Reasons to Hire
✅ **Overwhelmed**: Too complex, stressed  
✅ **Large inheritance**: Estate planning needed  
✅ **Significant wealth**: $500k+ needs strategy  
✅ **Business owner**: Complicated tax situation  
✅ **Major life change**: Divorce, retirement, death  
✅ **Poor discipline**: Will help you stay on plan  

### Bad Reasons (Don't Pay For)
❌ Manage small portfolio (<$100k)  
❌ Just want someone to tell you buy index funds  
❌ Can't find free advice online (tons available)  
❌ Like the person (find good advisor anyway)  

## Finding a Good Advisor

### Check Credentials
- **CFP (Certified Financial Planner)**: Rigorous certification (good)
- **ChFC**: Similar to CFP (good)
- **CFA**: Investment-focused (good)
- **Any 3-letter combo**: Could mean nothing

### Check for Problems
- Search SEC database: SEC.gov (Look up RIAs)
- Search FINRA database: BrokerCheck.finra.org (Look up brokers)
- Any disciplinary history?
- Any client complaints?

### Ask Key Questions
1. **"Are you a fiduciary?"** (Only yes is acceptable)
2. **"What's your fee model?"** (Want transparent)
3. **"How are you compensated?"** (Want to understand incentives)
4. **"What's your investment philosophy?"** (Want match with yours)
5. **"Can I see sample plan?"** (See their work quality)

### Get Interviews
- Interview 3-5 advisors
- Compare fees and approaches
- Pick best fit
- Not just cheapest

## Cost of Financial Advice

### Fee-Only
- Hourly: $150-$400/hour
- For complete plan: $1,000-$5,000
- Ongoing: Usually none (sometimes $2,000-$5,000/year update)

### AUM Fee
- 1% on $100,000 = $1,000/year
- 0.75% on $1,000,000 = $7,500/year
- 0.50% on $5,000,000 = $25,000/year
- Compounds over time!

### Examples

**Scenario A: $100,000 portfolio, 10% annual return**

Fee-only advisor, $3,000 upfront:
- Setup fee: $3,000
- Annual return: $10,000
- Benefit: $10,000
- Net: $7,000 gain

AUM advisor, 1% annual:
- Fee year 1: $1,000
- Benefit: $10,000
- Net: $9,000 gain
- BUT year 2 onward: $1,000/year forever
- Over 20 years: $20,000+ in fees

## What Good Advisors Provide

✅ **Financial plan**: Multi-year strategy  
✅ **Investment policy**: Written guidelines  
✅ **Tax strategies**: Tax-efficient investing  
✅ **Behavioral coaching**: Keep you disciplined  
✅ **Rebalancing**: Automatic portfolio adjustments  
✅ **Goal tracking**: Quarterly check-ins  

## Common Advisor Mistakes

❌ **Recommending loaded funds**: High commissions  
❌ **Over-trading**: Generates commissions  
❌ **Complexity**: Doesn't simplify, complicates  
❌ **Ignoring your preferences**: Doesn't listen  
❌ **Higher than market fees**: Should be 0.25-0.75%  
❌ **No written plan**: Should document strategy  

## DIY vs Hiring Advisor

| Scenario | DIY | Hire |
|----------|-----|------|
| Portfolio <$100k | DIY | Robo |
| Portfolio $100-500k | DIY | Consider |
| Portfolio $500k+ | Advisor worth it | Yes |
| Complexity: Simple | DIY | No need |
| Complexity: Complex | Hard | Yes |
| Discipline: Strong | DIY | Advisors waste money |
| Discipline: Weak | Risky | Yes |

## My Honest Take

**For 95% of people**:
- Index fund + auto-rebalance (DIY or robo-advisor)
- 0.04-0.25% fee
- Beats 85% of paid advisors

**For 5% with complexity**:
- Fee-only CFP
- Pay $2,000-$5,000 upfront
- Get comprehensive plan

**Avoid**:
- Commission-based advisors (conflicts)
- 1.5%+ AUM fees (too expensive)
- Salespeople pretending to be advisors

## Key Takeaway
Most people don't need paid advisor. Index funds + discipline beats expensive advisors. If hiring: Use fee-only fiduciary. Check credentials and avoid conflicts. AUM fees compound to huge costs. Better to DIY 0.04% than pay advisor 1%. Advisor value: Behavioral coaching (keeps discipline) + tax planning. For simple investors: Robo-advisor $250/year beats emotional mistakes.
