# Money Psychology: Why We Make Bad Financial Decisions

## Cognitive Biases in Finance

### Loss Aversion
Lose $1,000 feels 2x worse than gaining $1,000 feels good.

**Impact on investing**:
- Sell winners early (lock in gains)
- Hold losers too long (wait for recovery)
- Overall: Underperformance

**Solution**: Set rules before investing
- Don't panic sell during downturns
- Rebalance regardless of feelings

### Overconfidence Bias
Most people think they're better than average.

**Statistics**:
- 90% of people think above-average drivers
- 90% of investors think they'll beat market
- Reality: 85-90% underperform

**Impact**: Trade too much, pick bad stocks, underperform

**Solution**: Index funds remove emotion

### Recency Bias
Recent events seem more important than they are.

**Example**:
- Bull market last 3 years: "Stocks only go up!"
- Bear market last 3 months: "Stocks only go down!"
- Reality: Both are temporary

**Impact**: Buy high (after gains), sell low (after losses)

**Solution**: Long-term perspective (10+ year horizon)

### Anchoring Bias
First number heard becomes reference point.

**Example**:
- Stock bought at $50
- Now worth $30
- Feel like "losing" (anchored to $50)
- Miss next 50% gain (to $60)
- Sold at worst time

**Solution**: Forget your buy price, focus on future value

### Herd Mentality
Follow the crowd, ignore analysis.

**Examples**:
- 2008: Everyone's buying real estate → Market crashes
- 2000: Everyone's buying tech stocks → Market crashes
- 2021: Everyone's buying meme stocks → Market crashes

**Impact**: Buy peaks, sell bottoms

**Solution**: Contrarian thinking (when everyone else is scared = buy)

### Sunk Cost Fallacy
"I've lost too much to sell now" = Bad decision

**Example**:
- Bought stock at $100
- Now worth $40
- Think: "Can't sell, I'd lock in $60 loss"
- Hold hoping recovery
- Falls to $20

**Solution**: Base decisions on future value, not past cost

### Status Quo Bias
Do nothing because changing is hard.

**Example**:
- Have high-fee 401(k) fund (1.5% fee)
- Should move to low-fee index (0.04%)
- But: "Too much trouble"
- Cost: 1.46% × 30 years = 50%+ of returns!

**Solution**: Automate good decisions

## Emotional Money Decisions

### Fear
- Selling at market bottoms (2008, 2020)
- Missing next recovery (stocks up 50%+)
- Actually: Worst investment decisions

**Solution**: Have plan beforehand, follow plan

### Greed
- Buying expensive stocks (2000 tech, 2021 meme)
- Expecting unlimited returns
- Buying on margin (borrow to buy more)
- Lose everything in crash

**Solution**: Reasonable return expectations (10%/year realistic)

### Excitement
- Trading frequently
- Excitement feels good
- But: Cost $1,000+ in fees/taxes
- Underperform by 2-3%/year

**Solution**: Buy and hold (boring but profitable)

### Shame/Embarrassment
- Made bad financial decision
- Embarrassed to tell anyone
- Isolate → No help → Situation worsens
- Debt spirals

**Solution**: Talk to financial advisor, accountant, trusted friend

## Behavioral Financial Strategies

### Pre-Commitment
Decide in advance:
- "I will not sell if market drops 20%"
- "I will rebalance annually"
- "I will invest $500/month no matter what"

**Power**: Hard to break your own rule

### Automaton (Automatic)
- Auto-invest paycheck
- Auto-rebalance
- Auto-pay bills
- Removes emotion

**Result**: Follow good plan without effort

### Rules vs Feelings
**Before emotion strikes**:
- Write investment policy
- Document your plan
- Commit to plan

**When emotion strikes**:
- Read your policy
- Follow it
- Ignore feelings

### Time Delays
Want to buy speculative stock?
- Wait 1 week (sleep on it)
- 90% of time: Don't want it anymore
- Real ideas still look good after pause

### Portfolio Rebalancing
Automatic "buy low, sell high":
- Sell winners (sell high)
- Buy losers (buy low)
- Removes emotion
- Improves returns

## Overcoming Poor Financial Decisions

### Step 1: Awareness
Recognize your biases:
- Am I emotional right now?
- Is this FOMO (fear of missing out)?
- Would I make same decision in calm?

### Step 2: System
Create system that bypasses emotion:
- Automation (paycheck auto-invested)
- Rules (written policy)
- Accountability (tell friend your plan)

### Step 3: Education
Understand long-term principles:
- Market volatility normal (20-30% swings)
- Time in market beats timing market
- Compounding works over years
- Stay invested through cycles

### Step 4: Diversification
Reduces emotional volatility:
- Pure stocks: Swings 30-40%
- 60/40 stocks/bonds: Swings 15-20%
- Feel less emotion, sleep better

### Step 5: Accountability
Tell someone your plan:
- Friend, advisor, spouse
- They can remind you when emotional
- "Remember our plan? Markets drop 10% every few years"

## Working With Your Psychology

### Risk Tolerance
How much loss can you psychologically handle?

**Too aggressive**:
- Portfolio down 40% in crash
- Can't sleep
- Panic sell at worst
- Lock in losses

**Too conservative**:
- Portfolio up 4% (bonds mostly)
- Inflation outpaces growth
- Won't reach retirement goal
- Anxiety different

**Right level**:
- Can sleep through downturns
- Accept losses as normal
- Don't panic
- Comfortable with volatility

### Personalize Your Approach

**If you're rational**:
- Index funds
- Minimal fees
- No research
- Set and forget

**If you like control**:
- 90/10: 90% index funds, 10% stock picks
- Learn, pick 5 stocks, hold 5 years
- Scratches itch without ruining returns

**If you need help**:
- Robo-advisor (automates)
- Financial advisor (keeps you accountable)
- Online community (peer support)

## Common Psychological Money Mistakes

❌ **Panic selling in crashes**: Locks in losses  
❌ **FOMO buying peaks**: Buy high, sell low  
❌ **Trading frequently**: Fees eat gains  
❌ **Overconfidence**: Underperformance  
❌ **Sunk costs**: Holding losers too long  
❌ **Herding**: Buying bubbles  
❌ **Status quo bias**: Staying in bad financial situation  

## Key Takeaway
Emotions are enemy of investing. Market drops 30%: Hard to hold. Market up 50%: Hard not to buy more. System beats emotion: Auto-invest removes choice. Written plan beats gut feeling: Follow your rules. Diversification reduces emotional volatility: Sleep better. Most investing success = Psychology (staying disciplined) rather than intelligence (picking winners). Boring strategy beats exciting trading. Time in market beats emotional timing. Warren Buffett's success: 90% psychology, 10% analysis.
