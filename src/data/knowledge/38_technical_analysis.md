# Technical Analysis: Reading Charts and Patterns

## What is Technical Analysis?

Technical analysis = Predicting prices using charts and patterns

**Core belief**: Past price patterns predict future prices

**Tools**:
- Price charts
- Support/resistance levels
- Moving averages
- Volume analysis
- Pattern recognition

## Does Technical Analysis Work?

### The Evidence
**Academic research**:
- Most studies: Doesn't beat random
- Some patterns: Marginal edge (2-3%)
- Professionals: Mostly underperform
- Random trading: Often matches professional results

### The Reality
- Some traders make money (survivorship bias)
- Many lose money (costs + taxes)
- Hard to distinguish skill from luck
- 95%+ of day traders lose money

## Common Chart Patterns

### Support and Resistance
**Support**: Price floor (bounces up)
- Stock drops to $50
- Bounces back repeatedly
- $50 = Support level

**Resistance**: Price ceiling (bounces down)
- Stock rises to $100
- Drops back repeatedly
- $100 = Resistance level

### Moving Averages
**50-day moving average**:
- Average last 50 days
- Smooths out volatility
- Trend indicator

**200-day moving average**:
- Average last 200 days
- Long-term trend

**Golden Cross** (bullish signal):
- 50-day crosses above 200-day
- Supposedly indicates uptrend

**Dead Cross** (bearish signal):
- 50-day crosses below 200-day
- Supposedly indicates downtrend

### Volume Analysis
**High volume**:
- Many traders agree
- Trend more likely to stick

**Low volume**:
- Few traders moving price
- Trend weak
- Reversal possible

### Candlestick Patterns

**Doji**:
- Open ≈ Close
- Indecision candle
- Reversal possible

**Engulfing**:
- Prior day: Small range
- Current day: Large range (absorbs prior)
- Reversal possible

**Hammer**:
- Small body, long lower wick
- Reversal possible
- Support found

### Chart Patterns

**Head and Shoulders**:
- Peak, valley, peak (higher), valley, peak (lower)
- Supposedly indicates reversal

**Double Bottom**:
- Two similar low points
- Supposedly indicates uptrend

**Triangle**:
- Price converging
- Breakout possible (up or down)

## Technical Analysis Skepticism

### Problems

❌ **Hindsight bias**: Easy to see patterns in past  
❌ **Pattern recognition**: Brain sees patterns everywhere (random clouds)  
❌ **Self-fulfilling**: Traders believing creates pattern (not fundamental)  
❌ **Sunk cost**: Traders hold losers "waiting for reversal"  
❌ **Costs**: Trading costs eat most gains  
❌ **Taxes**: Frequent trading creates capital gains  

### Academic Critique

"The number of publicly documented anomalies that challenge the Efficient Market Hypothesis has grown. However, most of these anomalies have limited economic significance and trading costs often eliminate most economic gains."

- Academic research consensus
- Technical analysis might identify patterns
- But practical profitability rare

## Day Trading Reality

### Typical Day Trader Experience

**Year 1**:
- Makes $100 trades (10x per day)
- 40% win rate ($60 * 40% - $100 * 60% = -$40/trade)
- 200 trades/year = -$8,000
- Plus taxes: -$10,000 net loss

**Why losing**:
- Commission/fees: $10-20 per trade
- Bid-ask spread: $5-10 per trade
- Need to be right > 50% to breakeven
- Most traders wrong < 50%

### Winners (5% of day traders)
- Started with edge (professional, capital, data)
- Years of experience
- Disciplined risk management
- Full-time focus
- Still only beat market slightly

## Technical vs Fundamental Analysis

### Technical (Charts)
**Pros**:
- Easy to learn (visual)
- Can trade short-term
- React to news quickly

**Cons**:
- Doesn't tell you quality
- Costs eat profits
- Millions of traders doing same thing

### Fundamental (Business Analysis)
**Pros**:
- Analyze real business
- Long-term approach
- Less competition (fewer analyze deeply)

**Cons**:
- Takes research time
- Wrong timing still loses
- Market irrational short-term

## Should You Do Technical Analysis?

### If You Trade
- Understand costs eat gains
- Most traders lose money
- Need edge (data, speed, capital)
- Average: -3% return/year

### If You Invest
- Technical analysis irrelevant
- Buy quality at discount
- Hold 5+ years
- Ignore short-term charts
- Long-term: +10% typical

## Technical Analysis for Confirmation Only

**Reasonable approach**:
1. Fundamental analysis: Find good company, undervalued
2. Technical analysis: Confirm entry (support level, not resistance)
3. Buy at support: Use chart to enter good opportunity
4. Hold long-term: Ignore daily fluctuations

**Example**:
- Company fundamentals: Good (undervalued)
- Chart: At 50-day support
- Action: Buy on support bounce (not at resistance)
- Hold: 3-5 years

This uses technical as confirmation, not primary analysis.

## Famous Technical Traders

### Successful Ones
- Jesse Livermore: Legendary trader (early 1900s)
- Stan Weinstein: Pattern trader (1980s-90s)
- But: Sample bias (we hear about winners)
- Reality: 95% lose money

## Key Takeaway
Technical analysis looks for patterns in charts. Support/resistance, moving averages, patterns are real statistical observations. BUT: Most profitable edge disappeared (millions studying). Academic evidence: Doesn't beat random reliably. Day trading: 95% lose money (costs + taxes + difficulty). For investors: Technical analysis unimportant (fundamental is important). If interested: Use only to confirm fundamentals, not as primary strategy. Time spent on technical analysis could be better spent on fundamentals. Boring fundamental analysis beats exciting technical charting. Warren Buffett (master fundamental) vs technical traders (mostly broke).
