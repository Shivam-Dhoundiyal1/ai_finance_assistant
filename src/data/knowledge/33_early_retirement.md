# Early Retirement (FIRE): Financial Independence

## What is FIRE?

FIRE = Financial Independence, Retire Early

**Goal**: Accumulate enough to live off investments indefinitely

**Usually**:
- Save 50%+ of income (aggressive)
- Invest in index funds
- Retire in 10-15 years
- Live off 4% withdrawal

## The 4% Rule

### The Math
- Have: $1,000,000 invested
- Withdraw: 4% first year = $40,000
- Adjust for inflation yearly
- Should last 30+ years (95% success rate)

### Example
- Want to spend: $50,000/year
- Need invested: $50,000 ÷ 0.04 = $1,250,000

### The Study
Research on historical returns:
- 50 years of market data
- Various withdrawal rates tested
- 4% rule: 95% of scenarios successful
- 3% rule: Nearly 100% successful

## FIRE Variations

### Lean FIRE
- Retire on $30,000/year
- Need: $750,000
- Timeline: 10 years
- Lifestyle: Modest

### Coast FIRE
- Save aggressively to age 35
- Then stop contributing at 35
- Let compounding work 30 years
- Live on earned income until 65
- At 65: Have $1M+ from no-work-required growth

### Barista FIRE
- Retire from full-time job
- Work part-time (health insurance + small income)
- Supplement with portfolio withdrawals
- Psychological benefit: Some work, more freedom

## How Long to FIRE?

### Savings Rate Formula
**Years to FIRE = 25 ÷ (Savings Rate %)**

Example calculations:

| Savings Rate | Years to FIRE |
|--------------|---------------|
| 50% | 50 years |
| 50% | 50 years |
| 60% | 16.7 years |
| 70% | 11.9 years |
| 80% | 9.4 years |
| 90% | 7.2 years |

**Reality check**: 90% savings rate unrealistic. 50-70% typical for FIRE seekers.

### Real Example: $100,000 Income
- Spend: $30,000/year (70% savings rate)
- Save: $70,000/year
- Time to $1M: 14 years (compounded)
- Retire at 39 with $1M+

## Getting to FIRE

### Step 1: Track Spending
Know where money goes:
- Rent/mortgage: $1,200
- Groceries: $400
- Utilities: $200
- Entertainment: $300
- Total: $2,100/month

### Step 2: Cut Unnecessary
Ruthless cuts:
- $50 gym no one uses → Cut
- $150 streaming services → Keep 1, cut rest
- $200 eat-out → Meal prep instead
- $100 subscriptions → Audit, cut unused

**Target**: Reduce $500-1,000/month

### Step 3: Maximize Income
Increase earnings:
- Side gig: $500-1,000/month extra
- Ask for raise: 5-10% increase common
- Career change: 20-50% increase possible
- Real estate: Rental income

### Step 4: Invest Religiously
- $70,000/year in index funds
- VTI (100% stocks okay for 20+ years)
- Monthly automatic ($5,833/month)
- Don't stop, don't panic sell

## FIRE Challenges

### Healthcare
- Before Medicare (65): Need coverage
- Options:
  - Buy ACA (Affordable Care Act) plan
  - Budget $300-500/month
  - Tax credits reduce cost if "low income"
  - COBRA: 18 months of employer plan (expensive)

### Sequence of Returns Risk
- Retire with $1M, portfolio drops 30%
- Now have $700,000
- Withdrawing $40,000/year is harder
- Solution: Keep 2-3 years expenses in cash

### Tax Optimization
- In early retirement: Very low income
- Can withdraw $44,550 (married) tax-free (2024)
- Take Roth conversions (convert Traditional to Roth)
- Complex but worthwhile

### Boredom
- Some people: Work gives purpose
- Retirement: Need other purpose
- Hobbies, volunteering, projects help
- "Coast FIRE" keeps some work

## FIRE Lifestyle

### Typical FIRE Budget: $40,000/year
**Sample breakdown**:
- Housing: $1,200/month ($14,400/year)
- Food: $400/month ($4,800/year)
- Utilities: $150/month ($1,800/year)
- Insurance: $400/month ($4,800/year)
- Transportation: $300/month ($3,600/year)
- Entertainment: $200/month ($2,400/year)
- Miscellaneous: $300/month ($3,600/year)
- **Total: $3,250/month ($39,000/year)**

### Leaner Budget (LEAN FIRE): $25,000/year
- Housing: $800 (roommate, LCOL area)
- Food: $250
- No car/cheap transport
- Limited entertainment
- Healthcare: Minimal

### Comfortable Budget: $60,000/year
- Housing: $1,500-2,000
- Higher quality food
- More travel/experiences
- Better healthcare options

## FIRE Examples

### Example 1: Software Engineer to FIRE
- Age 25: Starts job at $150,000
- Saves: $90,000/year (60%)
- Growth: 10% annually
- Year 15 (age 40): $1,500,000
- Retires with $60,000/year spending (4% rule)

### Example 2: Teacher to FIRE
- Age 25: Starts job at $45,000
- Can't save 60% on salary
- Partners with high-earner (combined $150,000)
- Saves $70,000/year (46%)
- Year 20 (age 45): $1,500,000
- Retires both

### Example 3: Coast FIRE
- Age 35: $250,000 invested
- Stops contributing
- 8% growth, 30 years
- Age 65: $2,500,000 (even without new contributions!)
- Keep working, live on salary
- At 65 have fortune

## FIRE vs Regular Retirement

| Aspect | FIRE | Regular |
|--------|------|---------|
| Retirement age | 40-50 | 65 |
| Savings rate | 50-70% | 10-20% |
| Years saving | 10-15 | 40 |
| Required portfolio | $1M | $2M+ |
| Discipline | Extreme | Moderate |
| Lifestyle | Frugal | Normal |
| Risk | Sequence risk | Longevity risk |

✅ Motivated by number
✅ Willing to live frugally
✅ Have stable high income
✅ Love idea of early retirement

### FIRE is hard if:

❌ Don't enjoy frugal living
❌ Income unstable
❌ Value lifestyle while earning
❌ Worried about healthcare

## FIRE Math Reality Check

For $1M portfolio:
- Needs $1M × 0.04 = $40,000/year
- 70% savings rate on $100,000 income = 10 years save
- Plus compounding = 12-14 years real timeline
- Works if you stick to plan

## Key Takeaway
FIRE requires 50%+ savings rate and index fund investing. 4% rule gives $40,000/year per $1M invested. Takes 10-15 years to reach $1M for high earners. Coast FIRE more realistic: Save to 35, let compound 30 years. Healthcare and sequence risk are key challenges. FIRE possible but requires extreme discipline. Regular retirement (65) less extreme but requires working longer. Choose based on personality and values.
