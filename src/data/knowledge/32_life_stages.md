# Financial Wellness by Life Stage

## Your 20s: Build Foundation

### Money Goals
1. **Create budget**
2. **Build emergency fund**
3. **Start 401(k)**
4. **Manage debt**

### Money Moves

**Maximize 401(k)**:
- Start immediately (time is money)
- If employer matches: Must do it (free money)
- Even $100/month at age 25 = $300,000+ at 65

**Build emergency fund**:
- Save $1,000 first
- Then 3-6 months expenses
- Takes priority over debt (except high-interest)

**Debt management**:
- Pay minimums on student loans
- Eliminate credit card debt ASAP (18% interest)
- Don't buy car with debt (takes 5-7 years)

**Start investing**:
- 401(k) first (employer match)
- Roth IRA next ($7,000/year)
- Index funds (VTI, VOO)

### Sample Portfolio (Age 25)
- 401(k): $10,000/year → 30 years = $1.5M
- Roth IRA: $7,000/year → 30 years = $1.0M
- Total: $2.5M+ just from regular contributions!

## Your 30s: Accelerate

### Money Goals
1. **Maximize retirement savings**
2. **Build wealth**
3. **Plan for major expenses**
4. **Own home (maybe)**

### Money Moves

**Increase 401(k)**:
- Bump to 15% if possible
- Catch-up contribution if over 50 (bonus $8,000)
- Employer match crucial

**Aggressive investing**:
- Still young, take risks
- 100% stocks okay (30 years to retirement)
- Dollar-cost average saves emotion

**Start college savings**:
- 529 plans (tax-free for education)
- Start even $100/month (15 years = $25,000+)

**Buy home** (optional):
- If cost of living high: Okay to wait
- If cost reasonable: May be good investment
- 20% down payment to avoid PMI
- Fixed-rate 30-year mortgage

### Sample Portfolio (Age 35)
- 401(k): $15,000/year × 10 years = $250,000
- Roth IRA: $7,000/year × 10 years = $100,000
- Taxable brokerage: $200,000 (personal savings)
- Total: $550,000 net worth (reasonable)

## Your 40s: Consolidate

### Money Goals
1. **Protect wealth**
2. **Plan retirement**
3. **Fund college**
4. **Adjust allocation**

### Money Moves

**Shift allocation**:
- 80/20 stocks/bonds (vs 100/0 in 30s)
- Reduce volatility as retirement nears
- Protect downside

**Increase insurance**:
- Own home: Homeowner insurance critical
- Have dependents: Life insurance (30-year term)
- Have assets: Umbrella insurance ($1M/$300/year)

**College planning**:
- If 529 started: Should be $150,000+
- If not started: Catch up ($500/month)
- Public university cost: $120,000-180,000 total

**Retirement planning**:
- At age 45: Should have $250,000-400,000
- Target at retirement (65): $1,500,000+
- If behind: Increase savings rate

**Tax planning**:
- Max out 401(k): $23,500/year
- Max out Roth IRA: $7,000/year
- Tax-loss harvesting in taxable accounts

### Sample Portfolio (Age 45)
- 401(k): $20,000/year × 20 years = $600,000
- Roth IRA: $7,000/year × 20 years = $200,000
- Home equity: $300,000
- Taxable: $400,000
- Total: $1,500,000 (excellent)

## Your 50s: Prepare for Retirement

### Money Goals
1. **Verify retirement readiness**
2. **Finalize allocation**
3. **Plan Social Security**
4. **Max savings before retirement**

### Money Moves

**Max catch-up contributions**:
- 401(k): $23,500 + $8,000 = $31,500/year
- Roth IRA: $7,000 + $1,000 = $8,000/year
- HSA (if qualified): $4,150 + $1,000 = $5,150/year

**Adjust allocation aggressively**:
- Age 55: Maybe 60/40 stocks/bonds
- Age 60: Maybe 50/50 stocks/bonds
- Reduces volatility as retirement near

**Review withdrawal strategy**:
- How much can you safely withdraw?
- 4% rule: $1M portfolio → $40,000/year
- Verify this covers expenses

**Plan Social Security**:
- Delay to 70 if possible (+32% per year you wait)
- Calculate breakeven age
- Married couples: Coordinate strategies

**Healthcare planning**:
- Medicare eligibility at 65
- Consider HSA as healthcare fund
- Evaluate Medigap options

### Sample Portfolio (Age 55)
- 401(k): $25,000/year × 30 years = $1,000,000
- Roth IRA: $8,000/year × 30 years = $300,000
- Home equity: $500,000 (paid off/mostly paid)
- Taxable: $500,000
- Total: $2,300,000 (retirement secure)

## Your 60s: Retire

### Money Goals
1. **Transition to withdrawals**
2. **Minimize taxes**
3. **Enjoy retirement**

### Money Moves

**Take Social Security**:
- Age 62: ~$2,000/month (reduced)
- Age 67: ~$2,800/month (normal)
- Age 70: ~$3,700/month (increased)
- Married couples get 50% spousal benefit

**Implement withdrawal strategy**:
- Use 4% rule (safe withdrawal rate)
- $1M portfolio = $40,000/year spending
- Adjust for inflation annually

**Tax-efficient withdrawals**:
- Taxable account first (long-term gains taxed lower)
- Traditional 401(k) next (ordinary income tax)
- Roth IRA last (tax-free!)

**Transition portfolio**:
- More conservative (60/40 or 50/50)
- Can't recover from 50% loss in retirement
- Still need growth (30+ year horizon)

**Enjoy the fruits**:
- Travel, hobbies, time with family
- This is what you saved for!

### 20s

### 30s

- [ ] Net worth: $100,000+
- [ ] College savings (529) started
- [ ] Credit score: 700+

### 40s
- [ ] Net worth: $300,000+
- [ ] Retirement on track (25x annual expenses saved)
- [ ] Home equity: Significant
- [ ] Insurance: Adequate coverage
- [ ] Tax strategies: Implemented

### 50s
- [ ] Retirement readiness verified
- [ ] Net worth: $1,000,000+

## Key Takeaway
Early 20s: Start small, let compound work. Build emergency fund, max employer match. 30s: Accelerate savings, buy home if affordable, start college savings. 40s: Protect wealth, shift to growth, adjust allocation. 50s: Max catch-ups, verify retirement readiness, plan withdrawals. 60s+: Enjoy! Use withdrawal strategies, minimize taxes, reap rewards of decades of discipline.
