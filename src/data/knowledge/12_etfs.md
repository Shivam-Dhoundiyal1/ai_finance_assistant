# ETFs: Exchange-Traded Funds Explained

## What is an ETF?

An ETF (Exchange-Traded Fund) is like a mutual fund but trades like a stock on an exchange. It holds a basket of securities (stocks, bonds, commodities) tracking an index or specific strategy.

## ETF vs Mutual Fund

### Mutual Fund
- Priced once daily (after market close)
- Bought/sold through fund company
- Higher minimum investment
- Higher expense ratios usually
- Tax inefficient

### ETF
- Priced constantly (like stocks)
- Bought/sold through stock broker
- Can start with 1 share
- Lower expense ratios usually
- Tax efficient

## Types of ETFs

### Index ETFs (Most Popular)
- Track a specific index
- Low cost
- Passive management
- Examples: SPY (S&P 500), QQQ (Nasdaq), IVV (S&P 500 alternative)

### Sector ETFs
- Focus on specific sector
- Example: XLK (Technology sector)
- Higher concentration, more risk

### Bond ETFs
- Tracks bond index
- Lower cost than bond mutual funds
- More liquid than bond funds

### International ETFs
- Tracks international stocks
- Developed or emerging markets
- Geographic diversification

### Factor ETFs
- Select stocks by characteristic (value, growth, momentum)
- More sophisticated

### Commodity ETFs
- Tracks commodities (gold, oil, etc.)
- Inflation hedge
- More volatile

### Inverse/Leveraged ETFs
- Advanced strategies
- Not for beginners
- Can lose money quickly

## Why ETFs are Popular

### 1. Low Costs
- Expense ratios: 0.03% to 0.5%
- Example: SPY = 0.09% expense ratio
- On $100,000: Only $90/year in fees

### 2. Tax Efficiency
- Create in-kind redemptions
- Minimize capital gains distributions
- You only pay taxes when you sell

### 3. Flexibility
- Buy/sell anytime during market hours
- Like stocks
- Get immediate prices

### 4. Transparency
- Holdings disclosed daily
- Know exactly what you own
- Easy to research

### 5. Low Minimum
- Buy 1 share
- Start investing with small amount
- Example: $100 SPY share = invest $100

## ETF Selection Criteria

### 1. Expense Ratio
- Look for: Under 0.20%
- Index funds: Under 0.10%
- Passive funds cheapest
- Active funds: 0.5-1.5%

### 2. Assets Under Management (AUM)
- Bigger is usually better
- $100M+ is safe
- Under $50M can close
- Larger = more liquid (easier to sell)

### 3. Trading Volume
- How many shares trade daily?
- Higher volume = tighter bid-ask spread
- Can sell without big price loss
- Look for: 1M+ daily volume for popular ETFs

### 4. Tracking Error
- How closely does it track its index?
- Lower is better
- Usually very small for index ETFs

### 5. Fund Inception Date
- Longer history shows stability
- 5+ years is good sign

## Building a Portfolio with ETFs

### Simple 2-Fund Portfolio
- 70% VTI (US stocks)
- 30% BND (Bonds)
- Total cost: ~$100 invested = $0.03 in annual fees

### Balanced 3-Fund Portfolio
- 50% VTI (US stocks)
- 20% VXUS (International stocks)
- 30% BND (Bonds)

### Aggressive 3-Fund Portfolio (Young)
- 60% VTI (US stocks)
- 30% VXUS (International stocks)
- 10% BND (Bonds)

### Target-Date ETFs (Simplest)
**Vanguard Target Retirement 2050** (VFV):
- Automatically allocates based on target year
- Becomes more conservative as you age
- One-fund solution

## Tax-Loss Harvesting with ETFs

**Strategy**: Sell at loss, buy similar ETF (not identical)
- Lock in loss for tax deduction
- Stay invested
- Example: Sell VTI at loss, buy SCHB (similar US stocks)

## ETF Risks

⚠️ **Tracking Error**: Fund doesn't perfectly match index (usually tiny)
⚠️ **Bid-Ask Spread**: Cost to buy/sell (usually small)
⚠️ **Market Risk**: ETF value fluctuates with holdings
⚠️ **Complexity**: Some ETFs too complex for beginners
