# Interest Rates: What They Mean for Your Money

## What are Interest Rates?

Interest rate is the cost of borrowing money or reward for lending.

**Example**:
- Borrow $1,000 at 5% interest
- Pay back: $1,000 + $50 (5% interest) = $1,050

## Federal Reserve Rate (The Base Rate)

### What is It?
Rate Federal Reserve charges banks to borrow overnight.

Currently (2024): 5.25-5.50%

### Why It Matters
All other rates flow from this:
- Mortgage rates: Fed rate + 2-3%
- Credit card rates: Fed rate + 15-20%
- Savings accounts: Fed rate - 2-3%

## Types of Interest Rates

### Federal Funds Rate
- Central bank rate
- Affects all other rates
- Changes roughly every 6 weeks

### Prime Rate
- Banks' prime lending rate
- Usually Fed rate + 3%
- Used for mortgages, auto loans

### APY (Annual Percentage Yield)
- Savings accounts, CDs
- How much you earn

### APR (Annual Percentage Rate)
- Loans, credit cards
- What you pay

## How Rates Affect You

### Mortgages
**Rising rates = Higher mortgage payments**:
- Rate: 3% → payments affordable
- Rate: 7% → same house, 50% higher payment
- Fewer people can afford homes
- Home prices fall

### Savings Accounts
**Rising rates = Better returns on savings**:
- Rate: 0.01% → $1000 earns $0.10/year
- Rate: 4% → $1000 earns $40/year
- Incentive to save increases

### Credit Cards
**Rising rates = Higher credit card costs**:
- Rate: 18% → Very expensive debt
- Consumers pay more interest
- Credit card debt becomes more expensive
- People borrow less

### Auto Loans
**Rising rates = Higher car payments**:
- Same car, different payment
- Less affordable for borrowers
- Car sales decline

## Bond Prices vs Interest Rates

### Inverse Relationship
As rates rise → Bond prices fall (and vice versa)

**Example**:
- You own bond paying 4%
- New bonds now pay 6%
- Your bond worth less (stuck with lower rate)
- If you sell: Sell at discount
- Price falls ~10-15% per 1% rate increase

## Stock Market and Interest Rates

### Rising Rates (Bad for Stocks)
- Cost of borrowing increases
- Companies' profits decline
- Discount rate rises (future earnings worth less)
- Stock prices fall
- Example: 2022 (rates up 4%) → Stocks down 18%

### Falling Rates (Good for Stocks)
- Cost of borrowing decreases
- Companies' profits increase
- Discount rate falls (future earnings worth more)
- Stock prices rise
- Example: 2023 (rate cut expectations) → Stocks up 24%

## Economic Effects of Rate Changes

### Fed Raises Rates (Combating Inflation)
**Immediate effects**:
- Borrowing costs increase
- Saving becomes attractive
- Consumer spending decreases
- Stock market may decline
- Bond prices decline

**Goal**: Slow inflation without recession

**Risk**: Triggers recession

### Fed Lowers Rates (Stimulating Economy)
**Immediate effects**:
- Borrowing costs decrease
- Saving becomes less attractive
- Consumer spending increases
- Stock market may rise
- Bond prices rise

**Goal**: Speed up slow economy

**Risk**: Causes inflation

## Interest Rate Cycle

### 1. Expansion (Economy Growing)
- Fed keeps rates low
- People borrow and spend
- Inflation builds
- Fed eventually raises

### 2. Tightening (Fighting Inflation)
- Fed raises rates
- Borrowing expensive
- Spending slows
- Inflation falls
- But recession risk grows

### 3. Contraction (Recession)
- Economy slowing
- Job losses begin
- Demand drops
- Inflation falls fast
- Fed holds rates or cuts

### 4. Recovery (Easing)
- Fed cuts rates
- Stimulus encourages borrowing
- Economy begins recovering
- Cycle repeats

## Managing Interest Rate Risk

### If Rates Rising
- Reduce bond holdings
- Increase stock/real estate
- Refinance debt now
- Lock in low rates
- Avoid long-term bonds

### If Rates Falling
- Increase bond holdings
- Consider mortgages
- Bonds prices rise (good time to own)
- Lock in savings rates

### If Rates Uncertain
- Keep asset allocation stable
- Don't try to predict
- Diversify across rate scenarios
- Rebalance annually

### Portfolio Asset Performance by Rate Environment

| Rate Scenario | Stocks | Bonds | REITs | Cash |
|---------------|--------|-------|-------|------|
| Rising | Poor | Very Poor | Fair | Good |
| Falling | Excellent | Excellent | Good | Poor |
| Stable High | Moderate | Fair | Good | Fair |
| Stable Low | Good | Moderate | Good | Poor |

**Takeaway**: Diversification handles rate changes

## Key Takeaway
Interest rates are fundamental. Fed rate drives mortgage, savings, loan rates. Rising rates hurt stocks and bonds, help savers. Falling rates help stocks and borrowers, hurt savers. Diversified portfolio handles rate changes. Don't try to predict rates - diversify across outcomes. Bonds and stocks respond differently to rates - combined they're less volatile than either alone.
