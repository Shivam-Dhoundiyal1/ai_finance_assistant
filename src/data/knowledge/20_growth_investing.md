# Growth Investing: Investing in Future Potential

## What is Growth Investing?

Growth investing is buying companies expected to grow earnings faster than market average. You're betting on future profits, not current price.

## Growth vs Value

| Aspect | Growth | Value |
|--------|--------|-------|
| Focus | Future potential | Current value |
| P/E Ratio | High (20-50+) | Low (< 15) |
| Strategy | Buy winners | Buy undervalued |
| Time horizon | 5-10+ years | 3-7 years |
| Risk | Higher | Lower |
| Examples | Tesla, Netflix | Coca-Cola, IBM |

## Identifying Growth Stocks

### Earnings Growth Rate
**Year-over-year earnings increase** = growth rate

Example:
- Year 1: $100M earnings
- Year 2: $120M earnings
- Growth rate: 20%

**Thresholds**:
- > 20% growth: Excellent
- 10-20% growth: Good
- < 10% growth: Modest (not true growth stock)

### Revenue Growth
**Year-over-year revenue increase**

- High growth: > 20%
- Healthy: 10-20%
- Slow: < 10%

**Why it matters**: Revenue drives earnings, more stable metric

### Market Opportunity
Is the company's market growing?

**Example**: Software-as-a-Service (SaaS)
- Market growing 25%/year
- Company growing 30%/year
- Taking market share
- Excellent opportunity

### Competitive Advantages (Moats)
Does company have defensible advantages?

- Technology/Patents
- Brand loyalty
- Network effects
- First-mover advantage

## Common Growth Stock Characteristics

### High Reinvestment
Profits go back into growth, not dividends:
- No dividend (growth, not income)
- Spending on R&D
- Expanding market presence
- Building new products

### Upward Earnings Revisions
Analysts keep raising earnings forecasts:
- Company beats expectations
- Momentum builds
- Stock price rises
- Attracts more buyers

### Strong Return on Equity
Efficient at converting invested capital to earnings:
- ROE > 20%: Excellent
- Growing ROE: Even better

## Growth Stock Risks

### Valuation Risk
Stock price may not reflect future growth:

**Example**: Tesla
- Stock priced for 50% annual growth
- If growth slows to 30%: Stock crashes 40%
- Expected future just got cheaper

### Market Saturation Risk
- Market growth slows (company matures)
- Competition increases
- Margins compress
- Growth slows dramatically
- Stock sells off

### Execution Risk
Company needs to execute growth plan:
- New product launches may fail
- Expansion may underperform
- Management may disappoint
- Result: Stock underperforms

### Secular Risk
Entire industry may decline:
- Movie rental (Netflix) vs cinema (decline)
- Smartphones vs BlackBerry
- Technology can quickly obsolete old industries

## Growth Investing Strategy

### 1. Find Growth Sectors
Identify industries with above-market growth:
- Cloud computing
- Electric vehicles
- Artificial intelligence
- Renewable energy
- Healthcare technology

### 2. Identify Leaders
Find companies leading these industries:
- Market share leaders
- Product leaders
- Execution leaders
- Multiple leaders

### 3. Fundamental Analysis
Study the business:
- Can they maintain growth?
- How long until maturity?
- What's the path to profitability?
- Any competitive threats?

### 4. Valuation (PEG Ratio)
**PEG = P/E Ratio ÷ Growth Rate**

Example:
- Stock A: P/E 50, growth 50% → PEG 1.0 (fair)
- Stock B: P/E 50, growth 20% → PEG 2.5 (expensive)

**Threshold**: PEG < 1.5 is reasonable for growth

### 5. Position Sizing
Growth stocks volatile:
- 5-10% per position maximum
- 20-30 positions (diversification)
- Accept some will fail

## Growth Stock Characteristics

### Innovation-Driven
- New products/services
- Disrupting existing markets
- Technology advantages
- Continuous improvement

### Market Expansion
- Growing total addressable market
- Expanding into new geographies
- Acquiring competitors
- Entering adjacent markets

### Pricing Power
Ability to raise prices without losing customers:
- Brand loyal customers
- Switching costs
- Unique product
- Superior quality

## Famous Growth Stocks

### Amazon
- Founded: 1994
- Path: Online retail → Cloud → Digital advertising
- Growth rate: 30%+ annually for 25 years
- Stock: $3 (1997) → $180 (2024)
- Returns: 60x in 27 years

### Apple
- Founded: 1976
- Path: Computers → Phones → Ecosystem
- Maintained high growth for 40+ years
- Stock: $0.27 (1980) → $180 (2024)
- Returns: 666x in 44 years

### Netflix
- Founded: 1997
- Path: DVD rental → Streaming
- Disrupted entire entertainment industry
- Stock: $38 (2002) → $240 (2024)
- Returns: 6.3x in 22 years

## Growth Investing Cycles

### Growth Outperforms
- 2010-2020: Growth vastly outperformed value
- Tech boom driving growth
- Cheap money fueling growth
- Winners got huge returns

### Growth Underperforms
- 2021-2023: Growth lagged value
- Interest rates rising
- Profit-taking
- Valuation contraction

### Lesson
Growth and value alternate. Successful investors own both.

## Growth Index Funds

**For most investors**: Growth ETFs better than individual stocks

### Popular Growth ETFs
- **QQQ**: Nasdaq-100 (tech-heavy, growth-focused)
- **VUG**: Vanguard Growth ETF
- **IUVM**: iShares US Growth ETF
- **IVW**: iShares S&P 500 Growth ETF

**Advantages**:
- Diversification across 100+ growth stocks
- Professional selection
- Low fees (0.04-0.10%)
- Less risky than individual stocks

## Growth vs Value: Which is Better?

### Answer: Both
Optimal portfolio includes both:
- 50% Value
- 50% Growth
- Or 60% / 40%

**Reason**: They have different cycles
- Value strong when rates high / economy weak
- Growth strong when rates low / economy strong
- Mix reduces volatility

## Growth Investing Mistakes

❌ **Overpaying for story**: Buying at 100x P/E  
❌ **Ignoring valuation**: "Growth at any price"  
❌ **Single stock concentration**: Too much Tesla  
❌ **Ignoring execution risk**: Assumes success  
❌ **Momentum chasing**: Buying after already up 100%  

## Key Takeaway
Growth investing buys future earnings potential. Works for companies with competitive advantages and clear expansion paths. Higher risk but higher return potential than value. Index funds reduce individual stock risk. Optimal portfolio includes both growth and value. Time in market beats timing market, even with growth stocks.
