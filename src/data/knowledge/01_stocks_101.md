# What are Stocks? A Beginner's Guide

## Definition
A stock represents ownership in a company. When you buy shares of stock, you become a partial owner (shareholder) of that company. For example, if a company has 1 million shares outstanding and you own 100 shares, you own 0.01% of the company.

## How Stocks Work

### Stock Issuance
Companies issue stocks to raise capital for growth, expansion, or paying off debt. By selling shares, companies transfer a portion of ownership to investors in exchange for money.

### Ownership Rights
As a stockholder, you have:
- **Voting rights**: In company decisions (if common stock)
- **Dividends**: Share in company profits (if company pays dividends)
- **Appreciation**: Benefit if stock price increases

### Stock Price Determination
Stock prices are determined by supply and demand in the market:
- **High demand + Low supply**: Price goes UP
- **Low demand + High supply**: Price goes DOWN
- **News and earnings**: Can dramatically affect demand

## Types of Stock

### Common Stock
- Most common type of stock
- Provides voting rights
- Last in line for dividends (after preferred stock)
- Higher potential returns but higher risk

### Preferred Stock
- Fixed dividend payments
- Higher priority than common stock
- No voting rights
- Less risky than common stock

## Why Invest in Stocks?

1. **Long-term wealth building**: Historically, stocks provide 7-10% average annual returns
2. **Dividends**: Some stocks pay regular dividend payments
3. **Ownership**: Actual stake in company success
4. **Inflation hedge**: Stocks historically beat inflation over long periods

## Risks of Stock Investing

- **Volatility**: Stock prices fluctuate daily
- **Market risk**: Entire market downturns affect most stocks
- **Company risk**: Individual company may fail
- **Loss potential**: You can lose your entire investment
- **Emotional risk**: Market swings can cause panic selling

## Stock Market Indices

**S&P 500**: 500 largest US companies (represents US market health)  
**Nasdaq**: Technology-heavy index  
**Dow Jones**: 30 largest US companies  

## Key Takeaway
Stocks are ownership shares in companies. They offer long-term growth potential but come with volatility and risk. For beginners, starting with diversified portfolios (through index funds) is safer than picking individual stocks.
