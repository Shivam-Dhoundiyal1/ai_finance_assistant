# Value Investing: Buying Low, Thinking Long

## What is Value Investing?

Value investing = Buying quality at discount

**Key principle**: Buy dollar bills for 50 cents

**Process**:
1. Find undervalued companies
2. Analyze fundamentals
3. Buy at discount to intrinsic value
4. Hold long-term
5. Profit as market realizes value

## The Value vs Growth Debate

### Value Stocks (Cheap)
- P/E ratio: Low (10-15x earnings)
- Dividend yield: High (2-4%)
- Example: Mature companies (banks, utilities)
- Risk: Why are they cheap? (Bad business?)

### Growth Stocks (Expensive)
- P/E ratio: High (30-50x+ earnings)
- Dividend yield: Low (0-1%)
- Example: Tech companies (high growth)
- Risk: Expensive, growth could disappoint

### Value Investing Philosophy
**Warren Buffett approach**:
- Buy quality businesses
- At reasonable/cheap prices
- Wait for others to recognize value
- Profit appears over years

## Finding Undervalued Stocks

### Key Metrics

**P/E Ratio** (Price-to-Earnings)
- Stock price ÷ Annual earnings per share
- Low P/E = Cheap (potentially)
- Example: $50 stock, $5 earnings = 10x P/E (cheap)

**Price-to-Book** (P/B)
- Stock price ÷ Book value per share
- Low P/B = Asset-cheap
- Example: Stock $50, Book value $100/share = 0.5x (super cheap)

**Price-to-Sales** (P/S)
- Stock price ÷ Sales per share
- Hard to manipulate (earnings can be manipulated)
- Low P/S = Cheap

**Free Cash Flow**
- Cash generated by business
- More reliable than earnings
- High FCF yield = Attractive

### Screening Process
1. **P/E < 15**: Not too expensive
2. **P/B < 1.5**: Not overpriced
3. **Debt-to-equity < 2**: Not overleveraged
4. **ROE > 10%**: Profitable
5. **Dividend yield > 2%**: Income or buybacks

## Value Traps (Avoid!)

### False Value
Sometimes cheap isn't good:

**Decline Trap**:
- Stock down 70% = "Cheap!"
- But business declining
- Will go to zero
- Don't catch falling knives

**Value Trap Warning Signs**:
- ❌ Declining revenue (customers leaving)
- ❌ Shrinking margins (losing competitiveness)
- ❌ Rising debt (financial stress)
- ❌ Leaving industry (changing business)
- ❌ Paying more dividend than cash flow (unsustainable)

### Examples of Value Traps
- Blockbuster (cheap but died to Netflix)
- GE (cheap but declining quality)
- HP (cheap but losing to competition)

**Lesson**: Cheap + declining = Worse investment than growth

## Moats: Competitive Advantages

Warren Buffett loves "moats":

### Types of Moats

**Brand Moat**:
- Coca-Cola (brand power)
- Can charge premium
- Customers loyal
- Hard to replicate

**Network Moat**:
- Facebook (1 billion users)
- More users = more valuable
- Switching costs high

**Switching Cost Moat**:
- Microsoft (software integration)
- Hard to switch from Windows/Office
- Enterprise software often has this

**Cost Moat**:
- Walmart (efficiency)
- Lowest cost producer
- Can undercut competitors

**Patent Moat**:
- Pharmaceutical companies
- Patent protection (10-20 years)
- No generic competition

## Value Investing Process

### Step 1: Find Candidate
Look for:
- P/E < 15
- Strong business
- Sustainable moat
- Known company

### Step 2: Analyze Business
Ask:
- How does it make money?
- Who buys the product?
- Who are competitors?
- Is moat sustainable?
- Is management good?

### Step 3: Calculate Intrinsic Value
**Discounted Cash Flow**:
- Estimate future free cash flows
- Discount back to present (8-10% rate)
- This is intrinsic value

**Example**:
- Company FCF: $100M/year forever
- Discount at 10%: Worth $1,000M
- Stock price: $800M market cap
- Margin of safety: 20% (good!)

### Step 4: Check Margin of Safety
- Intrinsic value: $20/share
- Stock price: $12/share
- Margin of safety: 40% (buy!)

- Intrinsic value: $20/share
- Stock price: $18/share
- Margin of safety: 10% (maybe)

- Intrinsic value: $20/share
- Stock price: $22/share
- Margin of safety: -10% (don't buy, too expensive)

### Step 5: Buy and Wait
- Buy at discount
- Hold 5-10+ years
- Ignore market noise
- Let compound work

## Famous Value Investors

### Warren Buffett
- Bought quality at discounts
- Held 20-50+ years
- Returns: 20%+/year (vs 10% market)
- Success: $130B net worth

### Charlie Munger
- Buffett's partner
- Same approach
- Same returns
- Success: $2B net worth at 100 years old

### Benjamin Graham
- Founder of value investing
- "Mr. Market" analogy
- Buy 50-cent dollars
- Wrote: "The Intelligent Investor"

## Value Investing Returns

### Historical Data
**Value stocks vs Market**:
- 30-year period: Value slightly ahead
- Recent decade: Value underperformed
- Because: Cheap stocks often tech-light

**Why Performance Varies**:
- In recessions: Value outperforms (stability)
- In booms: Growth outperforms (expansion)
- Over 20+ years: Both similar (~10%/year)

## Value Investing Strategy

### Pure Value Approach
- 100% diversified value stocks
- Low valuation (P/E < 12)
- High dividend yield (3%+)
- Buy undervalued, wait for recognition

### Value with Growth
- Mix of value + growth
- Some dividend income
- Some capital appreciation
- Less volatility than pure value/growth

### Individual Stock Value
- Research deeply
- Pick 10-20 best ideas
- Hold 3-5 years minimum
- Hope for 15%+ returns

### Value Funds
- Let professionals pick
- Lower research cost
- Vanguard Value Fund (VTV)
- Schwab Value Advantage
- SPDR Portfolio Value (SPLV)

## Value vs Index Investing

### Value Investing
- Requires research
- Requires discipline
- Requires patience
- Can underperform (if wrong)
- Potential: 15%+ returns (if right)

### Index Investing
- No research
- No picking
- No emotion
- Guaranteed: 10% returns (historical)
- Boring but reliable

## My Take

**For Most**: Index investing simpler, reliably beats most value investors

**For Interested**: Study Buffett, read Graham, pick 5-10 stocks, hold 5+ years

**Key**: Either way, buy quality, hold long, don't panic sell

## Key Takeaway
Value investing = Buy quality at discount, wait for upside. Requires analyzing fundamentals, finding moats, calculating intrinsic value, buying at margin of safety. Warren Buffett + Charlie Munger prove it works (20%+ returns 60+ years). But requires discipline, research, patience. Most people better off with index funds (10% reliably vs hoping to beat market). If you want to try: Start with 5 stock ideas, hold minimum 5 years, expect 12-15% if successful.
