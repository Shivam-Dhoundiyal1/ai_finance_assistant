# Long-Term Investing: Building Wealth Over Decades

## What is Long-Term Investing?

Investing with 10+ year horizon, ignoring short-term volatility, compounding returns. The strategy that builds real wealth.

## The Power of Time

### Compound Interest (Einstein's 8th Wonder)
"Compound interest is the eighth wonder of the world. He who understands it, earns it; he who doesn't, pays it." - Often attributed to Einstein

**Example: $10,000 invested**

| Years | 5% Annual | 10% Annual |
|-------|-----------|------------|
| 10 | $16,289 | $25,937 |
| 20 | $26,533 | $67,275 |
| 30 | $43,219 | $174,494 |
| 40 | $70,400 | $452,593 |
| 50 | $114,674 | $1,173,908 |

### Chart
Even small differences in returns explode over time!

- 5%: $114,000 after 50 years
- 10%: $1,173,000 after 50 years (10x more!)

## Why Long-Term Beats Short-Term

### Time Smooths Volatility
- 1 year: Could be +30% or -20%
- 10 years: Almost always positive
- 20 years: 100% of cases positive (historically)

### Compound Returns Explode
- Year 1: $100 → $110 (10% gain)
- Year 2: $110 → $121 (10% gain, but on $110)
- Year 3: $121 → $133 (10% gain, but on $121)
- Gains themselves generate gains!

### Market Always Recovers
- Crashes occur (2008, 2020)
- But always recover
- Longer horizon = always owned through recovery

## Long-Term Investing Examples

### Young Investor (Age 25)
- 40 years to retirement
- Can handle 80% stocks, 20% bonds
- Market crashes are opportunities (buy more)
- By retirement: Likely $1M+ despite crashes

### Early Retiree (Age 35)
- 30 years to 65
- Can handle 70% stocks, 30% bonds
- One year of losses is tiny in 30-year timeline
- Time fixes most problems

### Pre-Retiree (Age 55)
- 10 years to retirement
- Can handle 60% stocks, 40% bonds
- Volatility matters more now
- Less time to recover from crashes

### Retiree (Age 70)
- 20+ year horizon (potentially)
- But spending from portfolio matters
- 50% stocks, 50% bonds appropriate
- Need more stability

## Long-Term Strategy: Buy and Hold

### The Plan
1. Decide asset allocation (60/40, 80/20, etc.)
2. Invest lump sum or start dollar-cost averaging
3. Rebalance once/year
4. Ignore market news
5. Wait 30+ years
6. Become wealthy

### Why This Works
- No need to time market
- No fees from trading
- Tax efficient (long-term capital gains)
- Behavioral: You don't panic sell
- Compounding: Money works for you

## Common Long-Term Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Checking Portfolio Too Often
**Problem**: Daily/weekly checking increases anxiety
**Solution**: Check quarterly or annually only
**Result**: Less emotional, better decisions

### Mistake 2: Abandoning Plan in Downturn
**Problem**: Panic sell during crashes
**Solution**: Pre-decide: "I will not sell for 30 years"
**Result**: You own recovery

### Mistake 3: Increasing Risk Over Time
**Problem**: Win big, then increase stock allocation
**Result**: Lose in next crash, abandon plan
**Solution**: Lock in allocation based on age

### Mistake 4: Not Starting
**Problem**: "I'll start when I'm more ready"
**Reality**: Every year costs you compound returns
**Example**: Start at 25 vs 35 = 50% less by retirement

### Mistake 5: Switching Investments
**Problem**: Sell winners (lock gains), hold losers
**Solution**: Fixed allocation, annual rebalancing

## Sequence of Returns Risk

**Critical for long-term investors**: The order of returns matters

### Scenario A: Strong Finish
- Years 1-5: +5%/year
- Years 6-10: +15%/year
- Result: Strong wealth
- Reason: Most money growing at +15%

### Scenario B: Bad Start
- Years 1-5: -10%/year
- Years 6-10: +15%/year
- Result: Less wealth
- Reason: Less money to grow at +15%

### Implication
- Near retirees vulnerable to crashes (bad timing)
- Young investors immune (time to recover)
- De-risk as you age (less stocks nearer retirement)
