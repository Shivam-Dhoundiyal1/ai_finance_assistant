# Real Estate Investing: Building Wealth Through Property

## Why Real Estate?

### Advantages
✅ **Tangible asset**: You can see, touch, improve property  
✅ **Leverage**: Borrow 80% of value, control 100%  
✅ **Appreciation**: Historically 3-4% annually  
✅ **Cash flow**: Rent covers expenses + profit  
✅ **Tax benefits**: Depreciation, deductions, exchanges  
✅ **Forced savings**: Mortgage payment like forced investment  

## Types of Real Estate Investments

### Owner-Occupied Home
Live in property (you own), builds equity:

**Pros**:
- Tax deduction on mortgage interest
- Principal and interest payment builds equity
- Can borrow against equity (HELOC)
- Live for free (vs renting)

**Cons**:
- Illiquid (takes months to sell)
- Transaction costs high (6% commission)
- Maintenance/repairs on you
- Property tax, insurance costs

### Rental Property (Landlord)
Rent to tenants, collect monthly income:

**Pros**:
- Passive income (tenants pay mortgage)
- Appreciation while living elsewhere
- Depreciation tax deduction
- Leverage still applies

**Cons**:
- Tenants can be problematic
- Vacancy periods
- Maintenance emergencies
- Landlord liability
- More management required

### REITs (Real Estate Investment Trusts)
Buy stock-like shares of real estate companies:

**Companies own**:
- Office buildings
- Apartment complexes
- Shopping centers
- Hotels
- Infrastructure

**Pros**:
- Liquid (easy to buy/sell)
- Diversified (own 100s of properties)
- No management needed
- Low minimum investment
- Monthly dividends

**Cons**:
- No leverage (own what you pay for)
- Less control
- No depreciation tax benefit
- Stock market correlation

## Home Buying Fundamentals

### How Much House Can You Afford?

**Basic Rule**: 3x annual income
- Income: $100,000
- Affordable price: $300,000

**Better Rule**: Mortgage payment ≤ 28% gross income
- Monthly income: $8,333
- Max payment: $2,333
- On 4% rate 30-year: ~$560,000 home

### Mortgage Basics

**30-year mortgage at 4%, $300,000 home**:
- Down payment: 20% = $60,000
- Borrow: $240,000
- Monthly payment: $1,146
- Total paid: $412,560 (interest: $172,560)

**Key**: Interest early, principal later
- Year 1: ~90% interest, 10% principal
- Year 30: ~50% principal, 50% interest

### Down Payment

**20% ($60,000)**:
- Pros: No PMI, good LTV
- Cons: Large amount needed

**10% ($30,000)**:
- Pros: Less cash needed
- Cons: PMI required (~$100/month extra)

**5% ($15,000)**:
- Pros: Much less cash needed
- Cons: PMI + Higher rate
- When: First-time buyers, limited capital

**3% ($9,000)**:
- Pros: Very little down
- Cons: PMI significant, higher rate
- When: Desperate to get in market

## Rental Property Economics

### Simple Rental Example
**Property**: $300,000 apartment
**Rent**: $2,000/month = $24,000/year
**Expenses**:
- Mortgage: $1,150/month
- Property tax: $250/month
- Insurance: $100/month
- Maintenance: $300/month (10% of rent)
- Management: $200/month (10% of rent)
- Vacancy: $200/month (10% of rent)
- **Total expenses**: $2,200/month

**Oops!** Negative cash flow ($-200/month)

This is why market selection matters. Find:
- Good appreciation areas
- Strong rental markets
- Low property tax areas

## Real Estate vs Stock Market

### Stock Market (Index Fund)
- Returns: 10% average
- Volatility: High (30% swings)
- Liquidity: Instant
- Management: None
- Fees: 0.03%
- Leverage: Limited (margin risky)

### Real Estate (Owner-Occupied Home)
- Returns: 3-4% appreciation + equity build
- Leverage: 80%+ (control with 20% down)
- Management: Varies
- Liquidity: Months (not instant)
- Transaction costs: 6-10%
- Forced savings: Yes (mortgage)

### Real Estate (Rental)
- Returns: 3-4% appreciation + 5-8% cash flow
- Leverage: 80%+ (using OPM - other people's money)
- Management: High
- Tenant risk: Significant
- Maintenance: Unpredictable
- Tax benefits: Excellent

### Real Estate (REIT)
- Returns: 10%+ (approaching stocks)
- Leverage: None
- Liquidity: Instant (like stocks)
- Management: Professional
- Fees: 0.5% (competitive)
- Depreciation benefit: No

## When Real Estate Makes Sense

### Good fit:
- Want forced savings (mortgage payment)
- Can afford 20%+ down payment
- Have steady job (stable income)
- Want lifestyle benefit (own your home)
- Patient (10+ year horizon)

### Bad fit:
- Don't have 20% down (avoid PMI costs)
- Expect to move in 3-5 years (costs eat gains)
- Don't want maintenance responsibility
- Prefer liquid investments
- Can't handle tenant/landlord role

## Real Estate Investment Strategies

### Buy and Hold
- Buy property
- Live in it 5-7 years
- Appreciation + forced savings
- Move and rent it out (become landlord)

### House Hacking
- Buy duplex/triplex
- Live in one unit
- Rent others
- Others' rent covers your mortgage
- Free housing + appreciation

### Wholesaling
- Find undervalued property
- Contract to buy
- Resell contract to investor
- Quick profit, minimal capital
- Risky/harder than it looks

### Fix and Flip
- Buy undervalued property
- Renovate
- Sell for profit
- 3-6 month timeline
- Active business, risky

## Real Estate Financing

### Mortgage Types

**Fixed-Rate (Best for beginners)**:
- 4% for 30 years
- Payment never changes
- Predictable

**Adjustable-Rate (ARM)**:
- 3% first 5 years
- Then adjusts yearly
- Risk: Rates spike
- Savings: Initial payment lower

**Interest-Only**:
- 5% for 10 years (interest only)
- Then 20-year amortization
- Lower early payments
- Payment increases dramatically

**Rule**: Fixed-rate best for 95% of borrowers

## Tax Benefits of Real Estate

### Mortgage Interest Deduction
Interest on mortgage deductible:
- $300,000 mortgage at 4% = $12,000 interest year 1
- Tax bracket 24% = $2,880 tax savings
- Effectively paying 3.04% instead of 4%

### Property Tax Deduction
Some states allow property tax deduction

### Depreciation
Rental properties depreciate (on taxes):
- $300,000 building (land not depreciated)
- Depreciate over 27.5 years = $10,909/year deduction
- No actual money spent
- Tax-free income shelter
- Recapture when sell

### Capital Gains Exclusion (Home Sale)
Sell home at profit:
- Married: $500,000 excluded
- Single: $250,000 excluded
- If $400,000 gain on $500,000 sale: Pay $0 tax!
- Must live 2+ of last 5 years

## Key Takeaway
Real estate provides leverage, forced savings, and appreciation. REITs provide real estate without management burden. Owner-occupied homes build equity while you live there. Rentals provide cash flow if in good market. For beginners: Own your home, consider REIT portfolio instead of rental management headaches. Real estate long-term (10+ years) best strategy. Real estate vs stocks: Both good, diversify between them.
