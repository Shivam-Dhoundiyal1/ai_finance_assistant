# Small Business Finance: Running the Numbers

## Starting a Business: Financial Planning

### Initial Capital Needed

**Service Business (Low Capital)**
- Example: Freelance consulting
- Website: $500
- Software: $500/year
- Marketing: $1,000
- **Total: $2,000** to start

**Retail Business (Medium Capital)**
- Example: Online store
- Inventory: $5,000-10,000
- Website/platform: $1,000
- Marketing: $2,000
- **Total: $8,000-13,000**

**Manufacturing (High Capital)**
- Example: Food production
- Equipment: $50,000-200,000
- Space rental: $3,000/month
- Initial inventory: $10,000
- **Total: $100,000+**

### Business Plan Essentials

**Revenue Plan**:
- How many customers?
- What price per customer?
- Realistic first-year revenue?

**Cost Plan**:
- What are fixed costs (rent, insurance)?
- What are variable costs (materials)?
- What's the gross margin?

**Profit Plan**:
- Revenue - Costs = Profit
- When does business break even?
- When do you profit?

## Business Finance Metrics

### Gross Profit
Revenue minus cost of goods:

**Example**:
- Service sells for: $1,000
- Costs: $300 (materials, contractor)
- Gross profit: $700 (70%)

**Benchmark**:
- Service business: 70-80% margins
- Retail business: 40-50% margins
- Manufacturing: 30-50% margins

### Operating Profit
Gross profit minus operating costs:

**Expenses**:
- Salaries: $3,000
- Rent: $2,000
- Utilities: $500
- Insurance: $500
- Marketing: $1,000
- **Total: $7,000/month**

If monthly revenue $12,000:
- Gross profit: $8,400 (70%)
- Operating expenses: $7,000
- Operating profit: $1,400 (12%)

### Cash Flow vs Profit
**Critical**: You can be profitable but cash-negative!

**Example**:
- Sell $10,000 product
- Customer doesn't pay for 90 days
- You need to pay supplier today
- Cash problem!

## Financing Options

### Bootstrap (Self-Funded)
Use personal savings:

**Pros**:
- ✅ Keep 100% ownership
- ✅ No debt pressure
- ✅ Keep all profits

**Cons**:
- ❌ Limited capital
- ❌ Personal risk
- ❌ Slow growth

### Small Business Loan
Borrow from bank:

**SBA Loans** (Small Business Administration):
- Guaranteed by government
- 7-10% rates (better than commercial)
- $2-5 million typical
- Require collateral (home, business assets)

**Commercial Loans**:
- 8-12% rates
- Shorter terms
- Require strong credit

**Pros**:
- ✅ Infuse capital
- ✅ Keep ownership
- ✅ Tax-deductible interest

**Cons**:
- ❌ Debt obligation
- ❌ Requires repayment
- ❌ Personal guarantee likely

### Investors / Equity Funding
Sell ownership to investors:

**Angel Investors**:
- Wealthy individuals
- 5-25% stakes typical
- $25,000-$250,000 typical
- Mentor + capital

**Venture Capital**:
- Professional investors
- 20-40% stakes
- $1M+ typical
- Aggressive growth pressure

**Pros**:
- ✅ Big capital infusion
- ✅ No repayment
- ✅ Expert guidance

**Cons**:
- ❌ Loss of control (partial)
- ❌ Pressure to grow/exit
- ❌ Diluted ownership

## Common Business Mistakes

❌ **No business plan**: Winging it kills businesses  
❌ **Not tracking finances**: Can't tell if profitable  
❌ **Over-hiring**: Salary expenses kill startups  
❌ **Ignoring cash flow**: Profitable but cash-empty  
❌ **No emergency fund**: One bad month = closed  
❌ **Paying yourself last**: Owner money is important  
❌ **Wrong business structure**: Tax/liability issues  

## Tax Considerations

### Business Structure
**Sole Proprietor**:
- Simplest
- You and business = same entity
- Liable for everything
- Income taxed as personal

**LLC (Limited Liability Company)**:
- Separates you from business
- Limits personal liability
- Income taxed as personal (usually)
- Good middle ground

**S-Corp / C-Corp**:
- Most complex
- Significant liability protection
- Separate entity
- More taxes to file

**Recommendation**: Start as sole proprietor, graduate to LLC at $50k+ revenue

### Deductible Expenses

**Home Office**:
- Percentage of home (e.g., 10%)
- Utilities, rent, insurance deductible (that portion)

**Equipment**:
- Computer, desk, software
- Deductible (sometimes depreciated)

**Education**:
- Business courses, conferences
- Fully deductible

**Travel**:
- Business trips deductible
- Commute not deductible

**Meals**:
- 50% of business meals deductible
- Entertainment more limited

**Vehicle**:
- Business mileage deductible
- $0.67/mile (2024)
- Track carefully

## Scaling a Business

### From $0 to $100k/year
- Do everything yourself
- Bootstrap usually works
- Focus on customers
- Minimal team

### From $100k to $1M/year
- Hire first employees
- Delegate non-core tasks
- Build systems/processes
- Tighter finances needed

### From $1M+ to $10M+
- Professional management needed
- Multiple teams
- Likely needs external capital
- Less hands-on possible

## Business Financial Statement

### Income Statement
**12-Month Example**:
- Revenue: $120,000
- Cost of goods: $36,000
- **Gross profit: $84,000** (70%)
- Operating expenses: $60,000
- **Operating profit: $24,000** (20%)
- Taxes: $4,800
- **Net profit: $19,200** (16%)

### Balance Sheet
**Snapshot of assets/liabilities**:
- Assets: Equipment $10,000, Cash $5,000
- Liabilities: Bank loan $8,000
- **Equity: $7,000**

### Cash Flow
**Cash in/out**:
- Month 1: Revenue $8,000, Expenses $7,000
- Cash change: +$1,000
- Ending cash: $1,000

Critical to forecast monthly cash flow, not just annual

## Quick Business Financial Checks

- [ ] Revenue sustainable? (Realistic customer count?)
- [ ] Margins positive? (Profit possible?)
- [ ] Cash flow planned? (Won't run out of cash?)
- [ ] Runway calculated? (Months to break-even?)
- [ ] Owner salary realistic? (Can you live on profit?)
- [ ] Growth planned? (How to scale?)
- [ ] Risks identified? (What could go wrong?)
- [ ] Insurance adequate? (Liability, health, disability?)

## Key Takeaway
Start with clear business plan and financial projections. Track finances obsessively. Gross margin more important than revenue. Cash flow more critical than profit. Bootstrap if possible (keep control), borrow if leverage needed, sell equity only if big opportunity. Most businesses fail due to cash flow (not profit). Deduct everything possible (tax efficiency). Scale carefully (each stage needs different approach). Good financials = better decisions.
