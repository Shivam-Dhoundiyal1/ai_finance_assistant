# Rebalancing Your Portfolio: Maintaining Your Asset Allocation

## What is Rebalancing?

Rebalancing is adjusting your portfolio back to your target asset allocation. As investments grow at different rates, your allocation drifts.

## Why Rebalancing Matters

### Drift Example
**Target**: 60% stocks, 40% bonds

**Year 1 Performance**:
- Stocks up 15% → $6,900
- Bonds up 2% → $4,080
- Total: $10,980
- New allocation: 63% stocks, 37% bonds

**Problem**: Too much risk now (63% vs 60% target)

## How Rebalancing Works

### Step 1: Identify Drift
Review portfolio:
- Current allocation: 63% stocks, 37% bonds
- Target allocation: 60% stocks, 40% bonds
- Deviation: +3% stocks, -3% bonds

### Step 2: Calculate Changes Needed
Based on total portfolio value ($10,980):
- Target stocks: 60% × $10,980 = $6,588
- Target bonds: 40% × $10,980 = $4,392

### Step 3: Rebalance
- Sell stocks: $6,900 - $6,588 = $312
- Buy bonds: $4,392 - $4,080 = $312
- Done!

### Step 4: Document
Keep records for taxes (if taxable account)

## When to Rebalance

### Time-Based (Recommended)
- **Annually** (most common)
- Check every January
- Takes 30 minutes
- Simple discipline

**Advantages**:
- Calendar reminder (easy)
- No emotional timing
- Minimal trades

### Threshold-Based
- When allocation drifts >5%
- More frequent trading
- More tax events
- Potentially costly

**Example**: Only rebalance if 65% stocks (vs 60% target)

### Hybrid Approach
- Rebalance annually, OR
- If drift > 10%, rebalance immediately
- Catches major drifts
- Minimizes trades

## Benefits of Rebalancing

### 1. Maintains Risk Level
- Prevents drift to more aggressive
- Ensures you don't take too much risk
- Important as you near retirement

### 2. "Buy Low, Sell High"
Rebalancing does this automatically:
- Rebalance forces selling winners (high)
- Rebalance forces buying losers (low)
- Classic profitable strategy!

**Example**:
- Year of market crash: Stocks low, bonds high
- Rebalance: Sell bonds (doing well), buy stocks (cheap)
- When market recovers: You own more recovered stocks
- Extra gains from rebalancing!

### 3. Discipline
- Removes emotion
- Automatic rule: Rebalance yearly
- No "should I" decision
- Just execute

### 4. Enhanced Returns
Research shows rebalancing adds 0.5-1% annual returns over 20+ years through buying low/selling high.

## Rebalancing in Different Account Types

### Taxable Brokerage Account
- Capital gains taxes when selling winners
- Tax-loss harvesting possible (sell losers)
- Strategy: Rebalance using new contributions
- Example: Contributions go to underweighted asset

### 401(k) Account
- No capital gains taxes on trading
- Rebalance freely without tax consequences
- Best place for frequent trading
- Recommended: Annual rebalance

### Roth IRA
- No taxes on gains ever
- Trade freely
- Best for active rebalancing
- Recommended: Annual rebalance

## Rebalancing Strategies

### New Contributions Method
Instead of selling, direct new money to underweighted:

**Example**:
- Portfolio: 65% stocks, 35% bonds (target 60/40)
- New contribution: $1,000
- Send $1,000 to bonds (not stocks)
- Gradually rebalances
- No taxes

**Advantage**: Avoid capital gains taxes

**Disadvantage**: Takes longer, need regular contributions

### Dividend Reinvestment Method
Redirect dividends to underweighted assets:

- Dividend paid in overweighted asset
- Reinvest dividend in underweighted asset
- Gradual rebalancing
- Minimal cost/taxes

### Forced Rebalancing
Sell and buy to exact target:

- Quick (one day)
- Works if any drift
- Only in tax-advantaged accounts
- Tax bill in taxable accounts

## Rebalancing Frequency Analysis

### Rebalance Annually
**Pros**:
- Simple (one date/year)
- Lower taxes/fees
- Historically works well

**Cons**:
- Can drift 5-10%
- Misses "buy low" opportunities

### Rebalance Semi-Annually
**Pros**:
- Catches bigger moves
- Better "buy low" opportunities

**Cons**:
- More time commitment
- More taxes
- More trading costs

### Rebalance Quarterly
**Pros**:
- Very disciplined
- Captures more low-buy opportunities

**Cons**:
- Very frequent trading
- High taxes/fees
- Usually not worth it

### Rebalance Whenever Threshold Hit
**Pros**:
- Catches major moves
- Prevents excessive drift

**Cons**:
- Hard to remember
- Random trading
- Requires monitoring

## Common Rebalancing Mistakes

❌ **Never rebalancing**: Portfolio becomes too aggressive  
❌ **Over-rebalancing**: Excessive taxes and fees  
❌ **Wrong asset allocation**: Rebalancing to bad target  
❌ **Emotional rebalancing**: Selling winners because "took profit"  
❌ **Forgetting to rebalance**: Drift increases over years  

## Example: The Power of Rebalancing

**20-year case study**:

**Scenario A: No Rebalancing**
- Start: $100,000 (60/40)
- Final: $435,000
- Return: 7.8%/year

**Scenario B: Annual Rebalancing**
- Start: $100,000 (60/40)
- Final: $451,000
- Return: 8.1%/year

**Advantage**: +$16,000 from annual rebalancing (3.7% total gain)

**Reason**: Rebalancing forced buying stocks cheap (2008, 2020 crashes)

## Rebalancing Checklist

- [ ] Once per year (pick date, e.g., January 1)
- [ ] Calculate current allocation
- [ ] Compare to target
- [ ] Identify gaps (what's overweighted/underweighted)
- [ ] Calculate transactions needed
- [ ] Execute buys/sells
- [ ] Document for taxes
- [ ] Update records

## Key Takeaway
Rebalancing maintains target risk level and improves returns through automatic "buy low, sell high." Annual rebalancing is optimal for most (minimal cost, good discipline). In tax-advantaged accounts, rebalance freely. In taxable, use new contributions or dividends to rebalance gradually. Research shows 0.5-1% annual benefit over time. Boring but powerful.
