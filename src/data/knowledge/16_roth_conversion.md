# Roth Conversion: Advanced Tax Strategy

## What is a Roth Conversion?

A Roth conversion is moving money from a Traditional IRA/401(k) to a Roth IRA. You pay taxes now but grow tax-free forever.

## The Decision: Convert or Not?

### When Conversions Make Sense

1. **Low-Income Year**: Freelancer with slow year
   - Minimal tax bracket impact
   - Convert $50,000 at low rate
   - Benefit: Future tax-free growth

2. **Early Retirement**: Before age 59.5, between jobs
   - No W-2 income
   - Convert while tax rate low
   - Strategy: "Roth ladder" to access funds penalty-free

3. **High Future Tax Rates**: Expect higher brackets later
   - Convert now (lower rate)
   - Grow tax-free
   - Withdraw tax-free (higher rate avoided)

4. **Estate Planning**: Leave to heirs
   - Roth → heirs get tax-free
   - Traditional → heirs pay income tax

5. **Medicare Costs**: MAGI affects Medicare premiums
   - Roth conversions don't increase MAGI
   - Can manage healthcare costs

## Roth Conversion Example

**Scenario: You have $100,000 Traditional IRA**

### Option A: Don't Convert
- Keep $100,000 Traditional IRA
- At retirement (age 70): Forced RMD
- RMD taxed as income
- Pass to heirs: They pay income tax on all

### Option B: Convert Now
- Convert $100,000 to Roth
- Pay $24,000 tax now (24% bracket)
- Keep $76,000 net (or pay from other money)
- Grows tax-free
- At retirement: No RMD, no taxes on growth
- Pass to heirs: Tax-free!
- Net at death: Much more than Option A

## How Conversions Work

### Step 1: Decide Amount
- Full conversion: All Traditional IRA
- Partial conversion: Just portion

**Example**: Convert $50,000 of $150,000 IRA
- Leaves $100,000 Traditional
- Moves $50,000 to Roth
- Pay tax on $50,000

### Step 2: Execute the Conversion
Contact your custodian (Vanguard, Fidelity, etc.):
- Request Roth conversion
- Specify amount
- Process typically takes 1-2 weeks
- Funds moved to Roth IRA

### Step 3: Pay the Taxes
- You receive tax bill/1099-R
- Must pay tax by April 15
- Two options:
  - Pay from separate funds (recommended)
  - Convert additional (less efficient)

### Step 4: Track Basis
- Track pre-tax amount converted
- Track taxes paid
- Important for future distributions

## Pro-Rata Rule: The Tax Trap

### What It Is
IRS pro-rates ALL Traditional IRA balances when converting.

### Example That Shows Problem
You have:
- $10,000 Traditional IRA (pre-tax)
- $90,000 Roth IRA (after-tax)
- Total: $100,000

You want to convert $10,000 of traditional to Roth:
- Expected tax: $2,400 (24% × $10,000)

But pro-rata rule says:
- Traditional = $10,000 / $100,000 = 10%
- Conversion is 10% taxable
- Other 90% tax-free
- Actually: 10% of any conversion is taxable
- Even if you convert from Roth bucket!

**Result**: Can't escape taxes easily with existing IRAs

### Avoiding Pro-Rata Rule

**Solution**: Roll Traditional IRA to 401(k) BEFORE conversion
- 401(k) balance doesn't count in pro-rata calculation
- Only IRA balance counts
- This "solves" the problem
- Requires access to 401(k) rollover (current employer usually won't allow)

## Roth Ladder Strategy

**Goal**: Access Roth contributions early (before age 59.5) without penalty

### How It Works
1. **Year 1**: Convert $50,000 from Traditional to Roth
   - Pay taxes
   - 5-year clock starts

2. **Year 2**: Convert another $50,000
   - Different 5-year clock
   - Keeps laddering

3. **Year 6+**: Withdraw from Year 1 conversion
   - No taxes
   - No penalties
   - Only taxable contributions waited 5 years

### Perfect for Early Retirees
Retire at 45:
- Can't access retirement savings (penalty)
- Use Roth ladder to create income
- By age 50, can access Year 1 conversions penalty-free
- By age 55, can access Year 10 conversions
- Creates bridge to retirement accounts

## Backdoor Roth (For High Earners)

For those with income too high for direct Roth contribution...

### Steps
1. Contribute $7,000 to Traditional IRA (no deduction)
2. Immediately convert to Roth IRA
3. Report as conversion (pay minimal tax)
4. Result: $7,000 in Roth that wouldn't be allowed otherwise

### Pro-Rata Problem Strikes Again
If you have Traditional IRA balance: Pro-rata rule ruins backdoor Roth!

### Solution
1. Roll Traditional IRA to 401(k) first
2. Then execute backdoor Roth
3. Clean conversion with no tax

## Mega Backdoor Roth (MEGA!)

After-tax 401(k) contributions (not Roth, not pre-tax):
- Many 401(k)s allow additional after-tax contributions
- Contribution limit: $69,000 - ($23,500 salary + employer match)
- Convert to Roth immediately
- Result: Up to $69,000 additional Roth space!

**Catch**: Plan must allow (not all do)

## Tax Considerations

### Immediate Tax Bill
- Must pay tax when you convert
- Can't use IRA funds (then less to grow)
- Pay from other money if possible

### ACA (Healthcare) Impact
- MAGI increases with conversion
- May affect ACA subsidies (healthcare)
- Can reduce subsidies by $2,000+
- Plan if using ACA

### Medicare (IRMAA) Impact
- MAGI affects Medicare premiums for high earners
- Roth conversion increases MAGI
- May increase healthcare costs $300-1,000/year

### State Taxes
- Some states tax conversions
- Other states don't
- Important consideration

## When NOT to Convert

❌ You expect lower taxes later (unlikely but possible)  
❌ You need the money soon (defeats purpose)  
❌ Pro-rata rule creates huge tax bills (unless solve it)  
❌ You'll lose ACA subsidies (healthcare costs offset benefit)  
❌ You'll breach MAGI limits for other tax benefits  

## Conversion Strategy Timeline

### Ages 20-35
- Minimal conversions
- Focus on contributions
- Long time horizon

### Ages 35-50
- Annual partial conversions
- Slowly build Roth
- Large growth years ahead

### Ages 50-60
- Strategic conversions in low-income years
- Build Roth ladder before RMD age
- Major push before RMD forces taxable distributions

### Ages 60-65
- Analyze: Convert or live off Traditional?
- Mega conversions in low-income years
- Finish ladder before RMD

### Ages 65+
- After RMD starts, conversions less attractive
- But still make sense for heir planning

## Key Takeaway
Roth conversions move money from pre-tax to after-tax accounts. Pay taxes now, grow tax-free, withdraw tax-free. Makes sense in low-income years, early retirement, or for heir benefits. Watch pro-rata rule (can solve with 401k rollover). Advanced strategy but powerful for tax optimization. Consider with tax professional.
